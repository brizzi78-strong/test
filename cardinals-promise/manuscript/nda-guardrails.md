# NDA Guardrails for the Pharma-Years Chapters

*Working reference for Rob — what a former rep can generally write about, and
where the lines are. This is practical guidance, not legal advice; before
publication, have a publishing attorney do a standard pre-publication review
of the pharma chapters against your actual signed agreements (Novo Nordisk,
AbbVie, C.R. Bard — each likely has its own confidentiality and
non-disparagement language, and the signed text controls, not the general
rules below).*

## Generally yours to tell

- **Your own story** — what you did, felt, learned, feared, and changed.
  An NDA protects the company's information, not your life.
- **Anything already public** — launch dates, list prices, FDA shortage
  listings and resolutions, indications, publicly reported events. The
  panel-review process is already verifying these against public record.
- **The texture of the job as commonly known** — sample drops and
  signatures, hallway calls, lunch-and-learns, prior-auth support, national
  sales meetings, quotas and rankings *as things that exist*. Thousands of
  reps have described this; "Hard Sell" (Jamie Reidy's Viagra-rep memoir)
  and many others published it.
- **Your own compensation and awards** in general terms (the plaque, "a
  bonus check I couldn't tell you the amount of" — the vagueness in the
  current drafts is the right instinct).
- **Composite patients and doctors with details changed** — already the
  standard across every chapter in this set. Keep it that way.

## Leave out (classic NDA territory)

- **Numbers only an insider would know**: quotas, territory sales figures,
  market-share data, targeting/call-list data, rebate or contracting
  strategy, manufacturing details beyond public reporting.
- **Internal documents verbatim**: training modules, talk tracks quoted
  word-for-word, internal emails, CRM screenshots, strategy decks. Describing
  that a talk track existed is fine; reproducing it isn't.
- **Unreleased or pipeline information** from your era, even if it later
  became public — tell it from the public record, not from what you knew
  inside.
- **Anything a competitor would find useful.** That's the cleanest test a
  lawyer will give you: if the only way you know it is from inside, and a
  competitor would want it, it stays out.

## Handle with care

- **Non-disparagement**: severance agreements often include it. The current
  chapters are safe by design — no company villain, the self-implication
  runs toward you, not the employer. Keep that posture.
- **Real colleagues** (Diabetes Dave, trainers, managers): not usually an
  NDA issue but a personal one — permission or disguise, especially for
  sacred material. The diabetes-dave draft already gates on his blessing.
- **Doctors and offices**: composites, no real prescriber names tied to
  prescribing behavior — that touches both privacy and PhRMA-code optics.
- **Hope's pharmacy stories**: her professional obligations, not your NDA —
  composites with details changed, her sign-off on everything in her lane
  (already flagged in the shortage chapter).
- **Patient information of any kind**: never identifiable, period. Already
  the practice; a book-level "encounters are composites" disclaimer is
  recommended.

## The one-line version

Tell your story and the public record; leave out the company's playbook;
change the names; get the two blessings that matter (Hope's and Dave's);
and pay a publishing attorney for one read before it goes to press.
