# Diabetes Dave

*The pharma years — the man who taught me what the disease actually costs.*

Everybody called him Diabetes Dave.

It was half a joke. It was also just the truth. He knew the disease better than anyone I ever worked with. Not sales-knowledge. Not slide-deck knowledge. He knew it in a way the rest of us didn't, and for a while I didn't ask why.

He was my friend. I worked alongside him in the years I was learning the difference between type 1 and type 2 off flashcards, and for a while I thought the knowledge was just mileage — a guy who'd been in the business long enough to know everything.

Then someone told me about his son.

Dave had a son. He was twenty-one when he died.

I'm not going to write what I didn't witness. A man I worked with buried his twenty-one-year-old boy. He was at work all the years I knew him.

Once I knew, I couldn't unsee it. He never rushed a question about a patient. He corrected details the rest of us would let slide. He had time for the newest, greenest rep at the end of the longest day. I was that rep. He never once made me feel like my not-knowing was a problem.

I don't know who gave him the name, or whether he chose it or just carried it. I never said his son's name to him. I don't know if anyone did.

He stayed. In the industry. In the rooms. In the details.

I think about the fathers I sit with now, in the worst weeks of their lives, and how some of them ask me — not in these words — what they're supposed to do with the rest of it. The grief. The time. Themselves.

I never know what to tell them. But I know what one man did.
