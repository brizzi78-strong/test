They flew a few thousand of us to Orlando and gave us lanyards and put Tom Brady on a stage.

That's what a national sales meeting is, if you've never been to one. A hangar-sized ballroom. Music too loud for nine in the morning. Your region's name on a screen the size of a highway billboard, and somewhere between the awards and the new quota, a famous person walks out to tell you about greatness.

I was still climbing that year. Close to the stage. Good seats were something you earned.

He was better at it than I expected. He told the story everybody comes to hear: six quarterbacks taken ahead of him, the 199th pick, the scouting report that said he couldn't move. How he kept the list of doubters. How he outworked the scouting report. Thousands of salespeople stood up and roared, and I stood up too, because I was a wrestler once, and that story lives in my body: being counted out, and staying anyway.

Then we all sat down, and he said something about team — I couldn't quote it now — and my mind did the thing minds do in arenas.

It left.

It went to a hallway in New Jersey. A doctor against a wall. A woman I'd never met who cried in a parking lot where everyone could see her and no one ever went. I never knew her name.

The greatest of all time was up there saying that nobody gets there alone, and back in New Jersey the people we existed for were sitting in fifteen-minute slots, apologizing to their doctors.

I stood. I clapped. I liked my plaque. The man on the stage earned every ring the hard way, and there are worse things to tell a room full of salespeople than *prepare like you're 199*. It wasn't his speech that unsettled me.

It was the roar. How easy it came. How good it felt.

I knew that roar from wrestling. The match happened at six in the morning, in a cold room, with nobody watching.

I flew home with a signed something in my bag and a new territory plan.

Years later, in hospice, I finally worked in rooms with no roar at all. Just a bed, a family, a window. Nobody claps.

Nobody gets there alone, he said. He was right.

Nobody leaves alone either.
