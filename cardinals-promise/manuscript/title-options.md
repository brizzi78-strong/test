# Title Options — the Ozempic Book

> **DECIDED (2026-07-30, final — Rob's pick):**
> **THE OTHER LEDGER — *An Insider's Story of Ozempic and the People the
> Numbers Missed***, standing as its own book in the Cardinal's Promise
> series. Front matter and chapter order: `00-front-matter.md`.
> Fallback for the publisher conversation if pushed: "Selling Ozempic —
> An Insider's Story of the Drug That Revolutionized Medicine — and the
> People It Couldn't Reach."

*Working notes on Rob's proposed title and alternatives; the trade-off map
that produced the decision.*

## Rob's proposal

**"Insider's Tale of Ozempic: The Drug That Revolutionized the World"**

What works: *insider* is the correct market position — the shelf wants an
inside account and nobody else on it carried the bag. *Ozempic* in the
title is commercially right (search, shelf, media bookings) and legally
workable — titles about a subject can name the subject (precedent:
*Hard Sell: The Evolution of a Viagra Salesman*, published, filmed);
a publisher's legal read confirms the final form.

What fights the book: **"revolutionized the world" is the drug's claim,
not the book's.** Sixteen panel-reviewed chapters have cut every line that
put the molecule at the center — the book's own thesis is that the drug
never meant anything; the people did. A hype subtitle promises the reader
a business/science saga and delivers a presence memoir; the mismatch costs
reviews. Also "tale" reads soft next to the material (a dead son, the
rationing years, a hospice kitchen table).

## The pattern that fits both needs

Human main title (the book's actual subject) + workhorse subtitle carrying
*insider* and *Ozempic* (the market's needs):

1. **The Other Ledger** — *An Insider's Story of Ozempic and the People the
   Numbers Missed.* The spine's own image (the dashboard vs. the uncounted
   ledger, "Samples, No Credit," the two-week lag). Strongest fit.
2. **Who Are You Worried About?** — *An Ozempic Salesman's Story.* The
   question that runs the whole book; risky as a cover (question-mark
   titles divide people) but unforgettable if it lands.
3. **Samples, No Credit** — *An Insider's Story of Selling Ozempic.*
   Rep-authentic, wry, carries the thesis. Reads slightly inside-baseball
   before you've read the chapter.
4. **The Question in the Hallway** — *How I Sold Ozempic, and What It
   Couldn't Sell.* Quietest option; closest to the memoir shelf, furthest
   from the news shelf.
5. **The Viagra of Diabetes** — *An Insider's Story of Ozempic.* Already
   flagged as a section-marquee candidate; as a book title it's the most
   commercial and the most joke-adjacent — it would need the interstitial's
   sober payoff to be the first page readers hit.
6. Straightest version of Rob's instinct, de-hyped: **Selling Ozempic** —
   *An Insider's Story of the Drug That Changed Everything — and the People
   It Couldn't Reach.* Keeps his shape; the em-dash turn does the book's
   turn.

## Recommendation

Option 1 or 6. Keep *Ozempic* in the subtitle either way; put the humans in
the main title. Test any finalist against the spine sentence — *ask the
question, then stay for the answer* — and against the last image of the
last chapter (butter pecan, ten in the morning). "Revolutionized the world"
fails that test; "the people the numbers missed" passes it.

## Open structural question (decide before the title)

Is this a **section of The Cardinal's Promise** or **its own book in the
Cardinal's Promise series**? Sixteen chapters with their own arc reads like
its own book. If it's the series' second book, the title should rhyme with
the series voice, not just the pharma shelf.
