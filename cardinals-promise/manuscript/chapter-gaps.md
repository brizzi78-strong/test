## The gaps, ranked

**1. The sober rep and the craving drug.** GLP-1s are showing real promise
against alcohol cravings — a placebo-controlled trial found semaglutide
reduced craving and heavy drinking, with larger trials underway. Rob has
been sober since January 9, 2009, and spent years selling this molecule.
The drug he carried may become an addiction medicine. Nobody else can write
this chapter. It's also the riskiest and most personal — entirely Rob's
call whether it belongs in this book or the next one.

**2. The last refill.** Nobody has written what happens to this drug at the
end of life: hospice deprescribing — the day the team gently stops the
medication a family spent years fighting prior auths for. The bridge chapter
between Rob's two careers, sitting in his exact lane, invisible to every
journalist on the beat. No research needed; only his patients' composite
stories.

**3. The insulin years.** Novo Nordisk is an insulin company; if Rob's bag
held insulin or Victoza before Ozempic, the book currently starts his story
one drug too late. The public arc is heavy: insulin affordability hearings,
rationing stories, the 75% NovoLog price cut (2023), Levemir's
discontinuation — and the Senate HELP hearing (September 24, 2024) where
his old company's CEO testified about Ozempic and Wegovy prices. A scene
option: the former rep watching his old CEO on C-SPAN.

**4. Ozempic babies.** The surprise-pregnancy wave (weight loss restoring
fertility; slowed absorption weakening the pill) — hundreds of women in
"I got pregnant on Ozempic" groups. The one unambiguous joy story in the
whole saga, and the set needs one: every drafted chapter is heavy. A
waiting-room or pharmacy-counter composite could carry it.

**5. The Oprah night.** "Shame, Blame and the Weight Loss Revolution"
(ABC, March 18, 2024): "I took on the shame that the world gave to me. For
25 years, making fun of my weight was national sport." The book's shame
thread going primetime — obesity named a disease, not a choice, to twenty
million people. Fits as a beat in the craze chapter (its redemptive
counterweight) more than as a standalone. Counterpoint to hold: eating-
disorder clinicians criticized the special's blind spots.

**6. What the drug became.** The label kept growing after Rob left:
cardiovascular risk (Wegovy, 2024), kidney disease (Ozempic, Jan 2025),
sleep apnea (Zepbound, 2024), cardiac events for the pill (Rybelsus, Oct
2025), Medicare coverage for heart patients (July 2026) — and the needle
itself is ending: oral GLP-1s arrived (orforglipron approved April 2026;
high-dose oral semaglutide filed). A short coda chapter: the woman who
feared the needle outlived the needle.

**7. The lawsuits.** Gastroparesis MDL ongoing — deliberately left
unadjudicated in the media chapter; revisit only when the litigation
resolves. Not a chapter yet.
