## CRITICAL FIX — the NOT must read as written IN, never crossed OUT

The brush strokes over "NOT" cannot form an X or strikethrough. At
thumbnail size a struck-through NOT makes the cover read "IT'S YOUR
FAULT" — the one catastrophic misread this design cannot survive, aimed
at a guilt-ridden buyer. Render NOT as an insertion: bold red scrawl,
underline, or caret — no crossing strokes. Squint test at 100px: it must
say NOT, unmistakably.

## Art Direction (REVISED)

Replace the father-and-boy with an adult daughter and her aging father.

The buyer of this book is her. She should see herself on the cover.

The scene: same bench, same golden-hour light, same hills. Her dad sits the
way old men sit — a little sunken, hands folded, looking out at something
only he can see. She sits beside him. Not posed, not smiling for a camera.
Tired. Maybe she came straight from work and her coat is still on. Her
shoulder leans toward him, or her hand rests over his. She is there. That's
the whole message of the image: exhausted, and still there.

She is roughly 45 to 60. He is 75 to 85 — thinner, white-haired, a cardigan
or flannel. The tenderness comes from posture and proximity, not expressions.

### Keep

- The painterly, brushstroke style — it matches The Cardinal's Promise brand.
- The cardinal on the branch, upper right, exactly as prominent as it is now.
- The warm sunset palette and the hills behind them.
- The navy band at the bottom for the author line.

### Avoid

- Hospital rooms, wheelchairs, medical equipment — this is a bench at golden
  hour, not a facility.
- Stock-photo smiles or a precious-moments glow — she is worn out and
  present, and that is the beauty.
- Making her look defeated. Tired, not broken.

## The One-Sentence Brief

If the designer needs the whole thing in one line:

> An exhausted daughter sits with her aging dad in the last good light of
> the day, and a cardinal watches from the branch — she's been telling
> herself it's her fault, and this cover crosses that out.
