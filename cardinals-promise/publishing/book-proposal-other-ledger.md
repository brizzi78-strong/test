## Overview

Before Ozempic was a punchline, a shortage, and a cultural revolution, it was a pen in a bag in the trunk of a car in New Jersey — and Rob Brizzi carried the bag.

He was hired to sell diabetes care without knowing the difference between type 1 and type 2 diabetes. He learned the disease off flashcards, learned the territory in fifteen-minute hallway conversations, and learned the job's real secret by accident: the question that moved more medicine than any pitch ever written — *"Who's the patient you're worried about?"* He rode that question to the number one ranking in the nation, a plaque, a bonus, and a night when his company closed Universal Studios so a few thousand salespeople could ride roller coasters in an empty park.

Then the drug got famous, and everything he'd sold changed meaning. Hope, his wife, is a pharmacist; the shortage emptied her shelves. The compounding Wild West put mystery vials in med spas, the monologues turned the word into a joke, and the headlines scared patients off medicine that was working. Rob watched it all from a new job — hospice — where one day, at a kitchen-table admission, a nurse's medication review reached the pen in the refrigerator door: the drug a family had fought prior authorizations, shortages, and a month of nausea to get, gently retired so a dying man could enjoy butter pecan ice cream at ten in the morning.

*The Other Ledger* is an inside account of the biggest drug story of this generation, and a book about the two ledgers everyone in it keeps: the one with numbers on it, and the one that counts. Rob's dashboard measured one drug, two weeks late. The other ledger — the insulin samples he stocked in closets for no credit while Americans rationed; the nurse who met him in a parking lot so a patient wouldn't hear her asking; the woman who stopped apologizing to her doctor — was the real story all along. It took a hospice job to teach him how to read it.

**One sentence:** The man who sold Ozempic, married to the pharmacist who dispensed it, now working in the hospice where it's let go — *Hard Sell* with *Being Mortal*'s heart.

- **Hard Sell** (Jamie Reidy, 2005) — the direct precedent: insider rep memoir, film adaptation; ours trades its comedy for stakes the Viagra story never had.
- **Magic Pill** (Johann Hari, 2024) — proof of the Ozempic book market; a journalist's testimony from the user's side of the counter — ours is the seller's, the dispenser's, and the deathbed's.
- **The In-Between** (Hadley Vlahos, 2023) — the hospice-memoir bestseller that proves Part III's shelf and this author's core audience.
- **Dopesick** (Beth Macy, 2018) — the pharma-rep moral machinery from outside; ours is the view from inside it, about a drug whose story is mostly one of benefit, not betrayal.
- **Being Mortal** (Atul Gawande, 2014) — the medicine-meets-mortality register the book closes on.
