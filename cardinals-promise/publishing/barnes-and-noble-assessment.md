# Barnes & Noble Assessment — The Other Ledger

*Two-agent panel, 2026-08-31: a chain category-buyer lens and a
table-browser psychology lens. Full agent reports summarized; decisions
flagged for Rob and the eventual publisher.*

## The draw, in one sentence

**The word OZEMPIC stops the hand; the credential ("the #1 Ozempic
salesman in America") gives permission to believe; the hospice reversal
creates the sentence people repeat.** The word-of-mouth line the whole
package should be built backwards from:

> "You have to read this — it's by the guy who was the #1 Ozempic salesman
> in the country, and now he works in hospice, helping families *stop* the
> drug he used to sell."

## Who stops at the table

- **A — the GLP-1 person** (pen in the refrigerator door): the traffic.
  Came for insider dope; gets mortality; the book is built as an A→B
  conversion machine. Package for A's curiosity.
- **B — the adult child of a dying parent**: the mission. "The Last
  Refill" is the best chapter in the sample for them — but nothing on the
  cover currently tells them the book is theirs. Seed them in the flap's
  last line.
- **C — healthcare workers**: complete delivery ("Samples, No Credit" is
  the book's most original reporting); not numerous, but the evangelists.
- **D — the exposé reader** (*Bad Blood*, *Empire of Pain*): the one
  browser the book will disappoint. Do not court in packaging; the
  betrayal reviews cost more than the sales.

## Where it lives, and how it gets a table

- **Section: Memoir** — the *Being Mortal* / *In-Between* shelf. Never
  Health (narrative dies next to keto workbooks). Primary BISAC: Personal
  Memoirs.
- **Tables are bought, not earned**: the realistic paths are (1) the
  **2027 Medicare semaglutide-pricing news peg** with one confirmed
  national media booking at on-sale — the single factor that most changes
  the order quantity; (2) staff picks (the hospice chapters are
  hand-sell bait); (3) BookTok ("sample prison" and the parking-lot
  insulin story are short-video-shaped).
- **Ship sooner, not later**: "Ozempic" is an enormous search asset with a
  decaying front-list half-life (successor drugs, the word going
  period-piece). The hospice half is the hedge — it earns the permanent
  backlist slot after the news value fades.

## The title tension (flag for the publisher conversation)

"The Other Ledger" **fails the spine test cold** (reads as
finance/thriller; pays off on page 92, not at six feet). It survives only
if the jacket compensates: **"OZEMPIC" set at near-title scale, and on the
spine itself**; the credential line on the front cover ("by the man whose
territory sold more Ozempic than any in America"). The buyer's blunt
verdict: *The Other Ledger* is the better book title, *Selling Ozempic*
the better product title — keep Rob's pick, arm the fallback.

## Back-cover copy (browser-agent draft, ~105 words)

> **He didn't know the difference between type 1 and type 2 diabetes — and
> he was about to start training.**
>
> Rob Brizzi talked his way into a job selling Ozempic before the world
> knew the word. Within a few years, his territory was #1 in the nation.
> He sold the drug in hallways, hauled life-saving insulin no dashboard
> counted, and watched the shortage, the compounders, the craze.
>
> Then he left it all for hospice work — where, at kitchen tables, he now
> helps families let his old drug go.
>
> He sold it. He watched it dispensed. He learned where it ends: butter
> pecan ice cream, ten in the morning, every bite wonderful.

**Never on the jacket:** "hospice" in the first half; exposé promises;
"memoir"; "grief"; series branding ("Book Two" reads self-published on an
unknown author's jacket — keep the series inside); the composites
disclaimer (endpapers, not flap).

## The first-page test

Rob's dictated first line **converts a cold browser** — confession, irony,
forward motion, and it flatters the reader (they don't know the difference
either). Keep the current opening; put the hallway exchange ("I don't have
time for a pitch." / "No pitch. One question.") on the **front flap** so
both hooks land in the 30-second window. Trim the industry-history
paragraph if anything — it's where the browsing eye slides.

## The honest numbers (buyer lens)

Debut memoir, modest platform: **initial chain buy ~1,500–4,000 units**
(1–3 copies/store, spine-out); **5,000–8,000 with face-out** if one
national media booking is confirmed at sales conference. Returns risk
35–50% is normal; ~8–12 weeks to prove velocity. Backlist is the genuine
strength — the hospice half restocks year-round like *The In-Between*.
Paperback at 12 months aimed at book clubs; the film option (*Hard Sell →
Love & Other Drugs*) is the lottery ticket.

## Weakness register

1. **Part III is thin for the title's promise** — "the people the numbers
   missed" gets ~2.5 chapters (one gated on Hope). Either Part III grows
   or Parts I–II surface their ledger-moments harder. A review problem
   that becomes a week-two table problem.
2. **Certification debt** — page one is the most scrutinized page in the
   book and cannot ship with stand-ins.
3. **The blurb that matters most**: one hospice-shelf name (Gawande if
   reachable; Hadley Vlahos as the plausible get; Puri, BJ Miller). One
   great blurb beats five okay ones; a pharma-shelf blurb (Macy) reopens
   the exposé expectation — strategically worse.
4. **The #1 credential must be exact and verifiable** before it goes on a
   jacket.
