# Jacket Copy Package — THE OTHER LEDGER

*Built from the B&N panel's findings (`barnes-and-noble-assessment.md`).
Everything here follows the panel's rules: OZEMPIC visible at six feet, the
credential on the front, hospice whispered not shouted, no series branding,
no exposé promises. All credential claims gated on verification.*

## Front cover architecture

- **Title:** THE OTHER LEDGER
- **Subtitle (load-bearing — set large, high contrast):**
  An Insider's Story of **OZEMPIC** and the People the Numbers Missed
  *(art direction: "OZEMPIC" at near-title scale — it is the second-loudest
  element on the jacket)*
- **Credential tagline (front cover, beneath author name) — two options:**
  1. **The arc (Rob's instinct, lead option):** *He started out not knowing
     the difference between type 1 and type 2 diabetes. Three years later,
     his territory sold more Ozempic than any in America.* (The humble half
     makes the proud half believable; it's the book in two sentences — and
     "three years" is anchored: start 8/2021, #1 in Q3 2024. If it runs
     long for a front cover, it's the flap's first line and the front
     carries option 2.)
  2. **The credential alone:** *By the man whose territory sold more
     Ozempic than any in America*
- **Spine:** title + "OZEMPIC" keyword (insist; spines don't show subtitles)
- **Design guardrail:** no teal-pen imagery mimicking Novo trade dress.

## Back cover (~105 words)

> **He didn't know the difference between type 1 and type 2 diabetes — and
> he was about to start training.**
>
> Rob Brizzi talked his way into a job selling Ozempic before the world
> knew the word. Within a few years, his territory was #1 in the nation.
> He sold the drug in hallways, hauled life-saving insulin no dashboard
> counted, and watched the shortage, the compounders, the craze.
>
> Then he left it all for hospice work — where, at kitchen tables, he now
> helps families let his old drug go.
>
> He sold it. He watched it dispensed. He learned where it ends: butter
> pecan ice cream, ten in the morning, every bite wonderful.

## Front flap

Opens with the hallway exchange, excerpted (the panel's recommendation —
both hooks inside the browser's 30-second window):

> *"I don't have time for a pitch."*
>
> *"Okay," I said. "No pitch. One question. Who's the patient you're
> worried about?"*
>
> *He stopped walking.*

Then one paragraph:

> Before Ozempic was a punchline, a shortage, and a cultural revolution,
> it was a pen in a bag in the trunk of a car — and Rob Brizzi carried the
> bag. This is the story of the most famous drug of our time, told by the
> one kind of witness it hasn't had: the man who sold it, married to the
> pharmacist who dispensed it, now working where it's finally let go. For
> anyone who has ever fought an insurance company for a drug — or sat at a
> kitchen table deciding when to let one go.

## Back flap — author bio (~75 words)

> in hospice care as a Certified Dementia Practitioner, and is the author
> of the *Aging Parent Cardinal's Toolkit*. He is married to Hope Brizzi,
> PharmD, who lived this book's shortage years from behind the pharmacy
> counter. He lives in [town, state].

## Blurb strategy (one great beats five okay)

Target, in order: Atul Gawande (*Being Mortal* — the reach), **Hadley
Vlahos, RN** (*The In-Between* — the plausible get, perfectly on-mission),
Sunita Puri, BJ Miller. Avoid pharma-shelf blurbs (Macy et al.) — they
reopen the exposé expectation the jacket is designed to close.

## Words that never appear on this jacket

"Hospice" before the flap's second half · "memoir" · "grief" / "loss" /
"end-of-life" · "the shocking truth" or any exposé cadence · "The
Cardinal's Promise series, Book Two" (series branding lives inside the
book) · composites/NDA language (endpapers).

## Gates before any of this prints

The #1 designation verified in its official form · the first line's
chapter certified as lived scenes (page one is the most scrutinized page
in the book) · Hope's approval of every mention of her · [X]/[town] brackets
filled.
