# Launch Plan — *It's Not Your Fault*

The strategy in one line: **this book doesn't get discovered, it gets handed
to someone.** Sell it where families are already in crisis, through the
professionals standing next to them.

---

## The tension to settle first

The strongest channel below — professional referral — runs on the author's
20-year healthcare career and personal network. A pen name (RLB) weakens it:
a discharge planner adopts a book because she trusts the person who wrote it,
and "RLB" gives her nothing to trust.

Three ways to hold both:

1. **Public byline RLB, private outreach as yourself.** You email colleagues
   as Rob, say "I wrote this," and they stock it. Amazon browsers see RLB.
   Works until someone connects them publicly — which is likely.
2. **Put the credential forward, not the name.** Market as "written by a
   Certified Dementia Practitioner with 20 years across home health, hospice,
   and senior living." Institutional trust without a personal name.
3. **Drop the pen name for this book.** Strongest for the professional
   channel; weakest for privacy.

Decide before outreach starts, because the emails read differently in each case.

---

## Channel 1 — Professionals who stand next to overwhelmed families (highest leverage)

One discharge planner who keeps five copies at her desk outperforms any
paid advertising, because she reaches families at the exact hour they need it.

**Who to approach**, in order of how well they match the book:

| Target | Why they fit | The ask |
|---|---|---|
| Hospital discharge planners / case managers | Ch. 10 + the observation-vs-admitted trap is their daily conversation | Desk copies; "give at discharge" |
| Home health intake & liaison nurses | Ch. 6–7 is literally their intake conversation | Copies for intake packets |
| Hospice admission teams / chaplains | Ch. 27–29; your own career | Family-support shelf |
| Senior living move-in coordinators | Ch. 8–9 compare exactly what families ask them | Copies in move-in folder |
| Elder law attorneys | Ch. 19–26 documents section | Waiting-room / client copies |
| Area Agencies on Aging, councils on aging | Their whole mandate | Bulk for caregiver programs |
| Church care ministries, caregiver support groups | Where caregivers already gather | Group buys |

**Revenue note:** this is also the real money. Organizations buy 25–50 at a
time; individuals buy one. Set up a bulk/institutional path (direct sales at a
discount beats Amazon margin) rather than sending them all to Amazon.

**The outreach sequence:** short personal email → offer one free copy, no
pitch → follow up two weeks later asking only "was it useful?" → then ask
about desk copies. Never lead with the ask.

## Channel 2 — Amazon itself

Most caregivers find this book by searching at 2 a.m., not by seeing an ad.
That makes the listing the marketing.

- **Listing copy, categories, and 7 keywords are already written** in
  `amazon-listing.md` (updated for the current title). Paste it in at setup.
- **Reviews are the whole ballgame.** 15–20 honest reviews in the first month
  moves the algorithm more than any spend. Source: beta readers
  (`beta-reader-kit.md`), the professionals from Channel 1, caregiver groups.
  Never buy or trade reviews — Amazon will pull the listing.
- **Editorial Reviews** — the two doctor quotes go in Author Central *after*
  the listing is live (they're already approved verbatim).
- **Price with print cost in view.** 276 pages in premium color is expensive
  to print; check KDP's printing cost before setting list price.

---

## Channel 3 — Where caregivers already talk

- **Facebook caregiver groups** — hundreds of thousands of members asking for
  exactly this book weekly. Rule: participate for weeks before ever
  mentioning it, and follow each group's promo rules or you'll be removed.
- **Caregiving podcasts** — a guest spot telling the Central Park curb story
  reaches thousands of the right people for free. Highest-yield unpaid media.
- **Libraries** — caregiver collections; requires IngramSpark (libraries don't
  order from KDP).
- **Local press / senior centers** — a local author with a caregiving guide is
  an easy story for community papers.

---

## What NOT to spend on

- **Billboards.** Nobody buys a caregiving workbook from a highway sign. It
  can't target the moment of crisis, costs thousands, and reaches mostly
  people who don't need it yet. The same money buys ~200 desk copies placed
  with discharge planners — who hand them to families *at* the crisis.
- **Broad Facebook/Amazon ads before reviews exist.** Ads amplify a listing
  that already converts; they don't rescue one with two reviews.

## Sequence

**Before publish:** finalize the pen-name decision · order printed proofs ·
line up 15–20 beta readers ready to review on day one.

**Publish → +30 days:** listing live with full copy and categories · reviews
land · first 10 professional contacts get a free copy · podcast pitches out.

**+30 to +90:** IngramSpark for libraries/bookstores · follow up with
professionals about desk copies and bulk · caregiver-group presence matures ·
first bulk order closed.

**Ongoing:** email list at thecardinalspromise.com (free worksheets → list →
launch news). Every workbook reader is a future *Cardinal's Promise* reader —
the workbook is the front door, the memoir is what's waiting inside.

---

## The measure that matters

Not rank. **Ask professionals whether they'd re-order.** A discharge planner
requesting a second box of 25 is the only signal that proves the book does
what it claims — and that signal is what makes every other channel work.
