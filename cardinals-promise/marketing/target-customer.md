# Target Customer — It's Not Your Fault

> The one reader every cover choice, ad, and email should be aimed at. When a
> decision is unclear, ask: *"Does this land for her?"*

## Who she is

**"Linda," 55–65. The daughter who became the one.**

- Her parent (usually Mom or Dad, **80–90**) has started to decline — a fall, a
  diagnosis, a hospital discharge, or a slow slide she can no longer explain away.
- Among her siblings, **she's the one who does it** — closest, most capable, or
  simply the one who can't look away. The others "help" from a distance.
- She may still be **working** (peak career or newly retired) and may still have
  **her own kids/grandkids** in the picture — the classic sandwich squeeze.
- She is competent everywhere else in her life, which makes feeling **lost and
  guilty here** especially disorienting. She is not falling apart in public; she's
  Googling at 11 p.m. after everyone's asleep.

## What she feels

Guilt she didn't earn. Second-guessing. Fear of doing it wrong. Resentment she's
ashamed of. Exhaustion. And under all of it: deep love, and the sense that nobody
handed her a map. **Our promise speaks straight to her: *you're not failing — you
just haven't been shown how.***

## Where she is (so we know where to spend)

- **Facebook — heavily.** This is her platform. Not TikTok, barely Instagram.
  Facebook feed + groups (caregiver support, "sandwich generation," Alzheimer's/
  dementia family groups) is the #1 paid channel.
- **Amazon** — she shops there, and she **reads the reviews** before buying. Reviews
  matter enormously for this buyer.
- **Google at night** — "what do I do with my aging parent," "how to care for
  elderly mother," "mom can't live alone anymore."
- She trusts **her doctor, her church, and an elder-law attorney** — which is exactly
  why the B2B (attorney) and faith channels convert.

## What makes her buy

- A cover and title that say **"this is for you, and it's not your fault"** — warmth,
  not clinical.
- **Credibility she can trust fast:** a Certified Dementia Practitioner who's been at
  the bedside (keep the CDP + hospice bio prominent — it's her permission to trust it).
- **Practical + doable:** checklists, "what to do this week," worksheets she can act on
  tonight. She's overwhelmed; she wants a next step, not a lecture.
- **Reviews from people like her.**
- Physical **8.5×11 workbook** she can write in and take to appointments — this buyer
  likes a real book she can hold and mark up.

## Her objections (answer these)

- *"I don't have time to read a book."* → It's built to be used on your worst day, not
  read cover to cover; find the right page in under a minute.
- *"Is this just going to make me feel worse?"* → The whole message is permission and
  a plan, not blame.
- *"Will it actually help or is it generic?"* → State-specific guidance, the Medicare
  traps, the real dollar clocks.

## What this changes in the plan

- **Meta ad age: 55–65 core** (test a 52–67 edge), **skew female**, **Facebook-first**
  placement — not the broader 45–65 we started with.
- **Voice everywhere:** talk to *her*, one tired daughter at 11 p.m., in the second
  person. Never "caregivers" in the abstract.
- **Reviews are priority #1 at launch** — she won't buy without them.
- **Lead creative** with the midnight-question hook and the permission line.
- **Gift angle (secondary):** she may buy a second copy for a sibling — "so we're
  finally on the same page." Worth one ad variant.
