# Content Pillars

Every post comes from one of these five. Rotate them. Never invent a sixth
on the fly — if an idea doesn't fit a pillar, it probably doesn't fit the
mission.

## 1. The Map (practical caregiving)

What it is: one concrete thing a family can do this week. Pulled from the
Toolkit — the Important Documents Folder, the observation-status trap, the
questions to ask a discharge planner, the RED/PURPLE/BLUE urgency system.

Test: could a stranded caregiver screenshot this and use it tomorrow?

Example seed:
> Before the crisis, one folder. POA. Insurance cards. Med list. Doctor
> names. One folder, one place, everyone knows where it is. That folder is
> worth more than every pamphlet the hospital will hand you.

## 2. The Unspoken Words (bedside language)

What it is: the exact words for the moment nobody prepares you for. The
daughter who hasn't said "I forgive you." The wife who doesn't know it's
okay to say "you can go." The patient afraid of being a burden.

Test: does this give someone words they didn't have an hour ago?

Example seed:
> If someone you love is holding on and suffering, they may be waiting for
> permission. It sounds like this: "We're going to be okay. You did your
> job. You can rest." You won't regret saying it. You may regret not.

## 3. Hospice Is Not Giving Up (the reframe)

What it is: dismantling the fear, one myth at a time. Hospice is the most
active form of love available. It says: I will not let you be in pain. I
will not let you die alone. I will show up every day until the last one.

Test: does this remove fear and install courage?

Example seed:
> "We're not ready for hospice" usually means "we're not ready for death."
> Nobody is. Hospice isn't the thing that makes death real. It's the thing
> that makes the time left livable.

## 4. The Story (Lou, the mat, the window)

What it is: one true moment from the memoir. The wrestling mat. January 9,
2009. The last week with Lou. Christmas Day. The cardinal on January 9,
2026 — seventeen years sober, fifteen days after Lou died.

Rules: show the moment, don't explain it. The cardinal is never decoded.
The number 17 is never turned into a lesson. Short sentences. Let silence
do work.

Test: would Lou recognize this as true?

Example seed:
> Lou didn't say much that last week. Neither did I. We didn't need to.
> I just stayed.

## 5. Behind the Promise (the work)

What it is: what it's actually like to walk into rooms most people avoid.
Hospice work. Sobriety as daily practice. What families teach you. What
the dying teach you. Love as a verb.

Test: is this witnessed, not performed? No hero framing. The families are
the heroes. Rob just shows up.

Example seed:
> People ask how I do this work. Wrong question. The question is how the
> families do it — and the answer is: one day, one visit, one hard
> sentence at a time. My job is to make sure they don't do it alone.
