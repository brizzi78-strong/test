# Content Library — Four Weeks, Ready to Post

Twelve base posts (Mon/Wed/Fri × 4 weeks) plus platform notes. Copy,
adjust if something real happened that day, post, log it in
`post-tracker.csv`.

Format per entry: **base post** (Facebook/LinkedIn length) → compressions
for Instagram caption, X/Threads, and a video beat for Reels/TikTok when it
fits. The book is never the subject. The link lives in the bio.

## WEEK 1

### Mon — The Map: the one folder

**Base post:**
Nobody picks the day they become a caregiver. It arrives as a fall, a
diagnosis, a discharge planner asking where your mom is going next — as if
you're supposed to already know.

You're not. But here's the one thing you can do this week, before any of
it: make one folder.

POA. Insurance cards. Medication list. Doctor names and numbers. Last
hospital paperwork. One folder. One place. Everyone in the family knows
where it is.

On the worst day, that folder is worth more than every pamphlet the
hospital will hand you.

**X/Threads:** One folder: POA, insurance cards, med list, doctor numbers.
One place. Everyone knows where. Make it before the crisis picks the date
for you.

**Video beat:** Hold up an actual folder. "This is the most important
thing in my parents' house." List the contents. End: "Make it this week."

### Wed — The Unspoken Words: permission

**Base post:**
Some people hold on longer than their body wants to. Not because of
medicine. Because of love. They're worried about you.

If someone you love is at the end and suffering, they may be waiting for
permission. It sounds like this:

"We're going to be okay. You did your job. You can rest."

You won't regret saying it. You may regret not.

**Instagram:** Carousel — Slide 1: "Some people hold on because of love."
Slide 2: "They're worried about you." Slide 3: "'We're going to be okay.
You did your job. You can rest.'" Slide 4: "You won't regret saying it."

**X/Threads:** "We're going to be okay. You did your job. You can rest."
If you've been looking for the words — those are the words.

### Fri — The Story: the last week

**Base post:**
Lou didn't say much that last week. Neither did I.

Twenty years earlier that silence would have scared me. I'd have filled it
with anything. Weather. Scores. Noise.

But I'd spent years in rooms like that one by then. I knew what the
silence was. It wasn't empty. It was full.

We didn't need words. I just stayed.

**X/Threads:** Lou didn't say much that last week. Neither did I. We
didn't need to. I just stayed.

## WEEK 2

### Mon — The Map: observation status

**Base post:**
If your parent is in a hospital bed right now, ask this question today:

"Is she admitted as an inpatient, or under observation status?"

Same bed. Same gown. Same IV pole. Completely different Medicare coverage.
Observation status can quietly void the rehab benefit — and families find
out at discharge, when it's too late to fix.

Ask today. Write down who answered and what they said.

**X/Threads:** Hospital question that saves families thousands: "Inpatient,
or observation status?" Same bed, different Medicare rules. Ask today, not
at discharge.

**Video beat:** "The question every discharge planner hopes you'll ask —
and most families don't know exists."

### Wed — Hospice Is Not Giving Up

**Base post:**
"We're not ready for hospice."

I hear it every week. And I understand it, because what it usually means
is: we're not ready for death. Nobody is.

But hospice isn't the thing that makes death real. The disease did that.
Hospice is the thing that makes the time left livable. It says: no pain we
can prevent. No dying alone. Somebody shows up — every day, until the
last one.

That's not giving up. That's the most active form of love available.

**X/Threads:** Hospice doesn't make death real. The disease did that.
Hospice makes the time left livable. That's not giving up — it's love with
a schedule.

### Fri — Behind the Promise: the wrong question

**Base post:**
People ask how I do this work. Walking into those rooms. Sitting with
those families.

Wrong question.

The question is how the families do it. And the answer is: one day, one
visit, one hard sentence at a time. The daughter who moves in. The husband
who learns to give meds at 80. The son who drives four hours every Friday.

My job is smaller than people think. It's making sure none of them do it
alone.

**LinkedIn note:** Post as-is. This is the pillar that lands with
providers and referral partners.

## WEEK 3

### Mon — The Map: the three colors

**Base post:**
Everything in a caregiving crisis sorts into three piles.

RED — act today. The fall, the discharge, the coverage question with a
deadline.

PURPLE — watch closely. The weight loss. The confusion that comes and
goes. The bills piling up unopened.

BLUE — plan ahead. The POA. The folder. The conversation you keep
postponing.

Families drown when everything feels RED. It isn't. Sort the piles, and
the week gets survivable.

**Instagram:** Three-slide carousel, one color per slide, book's color
system.

### Wed — The Unspoken Words: the burden

**Base post:**
"I don't want to be a burden."

If you love someone at the end of life, you will probably hear this. Here
is one way to answer:

"You carried me. Carrying you back isn't a burden. It's my turn."

They may argue. Say it again. They're not asking to be talked out of it —
they're asking whether it's true. Show them it is.

**X/Threads:** "I don't want to be a burden." — "You carried me. It's my
turn." They're not arguing. They're asking if it's true.

### Fri — The Story: January 9

**Base post:**
January 9, 2009. The day everything started over.

I've never been casual about that date. You don't get to be, when the
first years were that hard.

Seventeen years later, on that same date, I was standing at the window.
Fifteen days after we buried my father.

A cardinal landed on the branch.

I'm not going to explain it. I just know what I saw, and I know what date
it was.

**Rules note:** Never decode the cardinal. Never turn 17 into a lesson.
Post it plain and let the comments carry it.

## WEEK 4

### Mon — The Map: the discharge questions

**Base post:**
Discharge day moves fast on purpose. Here are five questions to slow it
down:

1. What exactly is the diagnosis going home with her?
2. What changed in her medications — and who reconciles the old list?
3. Who is responsible for her care starting tonight — name and number?
4. What would make you readmit her? What do we watch for?
5. What services is she eligible for — home health, palliative, hospice —
   and who orders them?

Write the answers down. The person taking notes gets treated differently.
That's not cynicism. That's twenty years of watching it happen.

### Wed — Hospice Is Not Giving Up: earlier

**Base post:**
The sentence I hear most from hospice families isn't about death.

It's: "We wish we'd called sooner."

Months of pain that could have been managed. Nights of fear with no number
to call. Conversations that ran out of time.

Hospice isn't for the last week. It's for the last chapter. And the
families who get the most from it are the ones who let it start while
there was still time to live inside it.

**X/Threads:** The most common thing hospice families say: "We wish we'd
called sooner." Hospice isn't for the last week. It's for the last chapter.

### Fri — Behind the Promise: love is a verb

**Base post:**
I used to think love was a feeling you waited to have.

Wrestling taught me otherwise first: you don't feel like practicing. You
practice. Sobriety taught me second: you don't feel like showing up to the
meeting. You show up.

And then hospice taught me for good. The daughter doesn't feel ready to
sit in that room. She sits in it anyway. That's not the absence of love.
That's the whole thing.

Love is a verb. It's what you do when the feeling isn't enough.
