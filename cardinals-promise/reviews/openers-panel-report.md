# Panel Report — "Type One, Type Two" and "Diabetes Dave"

Scenes: `manuscript/type-one-type-two.md`, `manuscript/diabetes-dave.md` ·
Draft v1s reviewed 2026-07-29 by a combined three-lens pass (craft, facts,
mission/sacred). Fixes marked **applied** are in the v2 drafts.

## Type One, Type Two — findings → what was done

| # | Finding | Outcome |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **"Nobody calls type 1 your fault" is false to lived experience** — T1 patients and parents face constant blame; the absolute throws them under the bus to sharpen the T2 point | **Applied** — the true asymmetry: "Type 1 gets its own cruelty — strangers still ask what the kid ate — but the doctors don't blame you." Also "often to kids, not only to kids" (half of T1 diagnoses are adults) |
| 2 | **"Genetics load the gun… free of charge, with refills"** — invokes the aphorism whose second half is "lifestyle pulls the trigger" (summoning the blame it rejects), plus a pharma pun performed on top of patient shame | **Applied** — cut; replaced with "some you can pick, most you can't; the blame doesn't make that distinction" |
| 3 | **The ninety-seconds paragraph duplicated the hallway chapter's opening** and universalized Rob's ignorance ("every one of us started in a parking lot") — self-grading | **Applied** — cut |
| 4 | **Closing couplet was a slogan** ("Not knowing was never the problem. / Not showing up would have been.") | **Applied** — cut; ends on "out loud, badly, until it's yours" |
| 5 | **The bluff redeemed too cheaply** ("it turned out to be the right training") with no cost acknowledged | **Applied** — replaced with the costly beat: "Somewhere in New Jersey there were patients on the other end of my bluff. I didn't think about them in the parking lot. I think about them now." |
| 6 | **"Confessing to furniture" congratulated itself** at a suffering man's expense (and doubled "apologizing to a clipboard") | **Applied** — cut; the beat now ends on the unanswered apology: "Nobody answered him. Not the front desk, not the room, not me." |
| 7 | Wrestling aphorism ("teaches you everything about the gap…") self-mythologized | **Applied** — only the dry first sentence survives |

Verified safe and kept: "nothing anyone ate or didn't eat caused it" (correct
T1 messaging); "without insulin from outside, you die"; Ozempic-for-type-2;
the parking-lot phone beat; "I studied like it was weight cutting"; "bad
this month."

## Diabetes Dave — findings → what was done

| # | Finding | Outcome |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **CRITICAL: the draft asserted his son died of diabetes** ("the disease that took him") — the anchor fact is only that his son died at 21. A fabricated cause of death for a real dead child is the worst possible sacred-story violation | **Applied** — the scene now asserts only what's anchored ("A man I worked with buried his twenty-one-year-old boy. He was at work all the years I knew him."); cause of death is a BLOCKING confirm item |
| 2 | **"Paid the full price" — commerce language for a dead child** inside a sales-industry piece | **Applied** — cut |
| 3 | **"He became Diabetes Dave." branded a grieving father** with a cutesy handle at maximum weight; the draft's own footnote suspected it | **Applied** — ends at "But I know what one man did." |
| 4 | **Stated moral prescribed a model for grief** ("you let it turn into the reason you're good to people" — grief owes nobody transformation) | **Applied** — cut; the concrete triad ("In the industry. In the rooms. In the details.") carries it |
| 5 | **The why-anaphora assigned Dave motives Rob didn't witness** | **Applied** — converted to flat observations |
| 6 | **Restraint performed as style** ("the fact does not need me") and a road-at-night simile doing coy foreshadowing | **Applied** — both flattened |
| 7 | **The missing confessions** — did anyone ever speak to Dave about his son? Did Rob? And who gave him the nickname — did he own it or endure it? | **Applied** — "I don't know who gave him the name, or whether he chose it or just carried it. I never said his son's name to him. I don't know if anyone did." — each gated in the confirm list as keep-only-if-true |
| 8 | "Twenty-some years" carried stand-in arithmetic with anchor confidence | **Applied** — dropped |

Protected on panel orders: "Dave had a son. He was twenty-one when he died."
— nothing may be added to or subtracted from those two sentences.

## Gating requirements (not suggestions)

1. **Dave's (or his family's) blessing before publication**; change the
   nickname and identifying details if out of touch.
2. **Cause of death comes only from Rob**, or stays unstated.
3. The "I never said his son's name" confession stays only if true of Rob.

## Structural option (both scenes)

Dave may be the true "moment the disease became a person" — the waiting-room
man in Type One, Type Two is a stand-in; if Rob's real moment was Dave, the
two scenes merge, with the waiting-room beat (shame) and Dave (cost) doing
distinct work only if kept separate.
