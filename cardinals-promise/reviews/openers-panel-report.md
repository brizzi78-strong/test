# Chapter Openers Panel Report — "It's Not Your Fault"

Four independent reviews of `book/chapter-openers.md` (16 drafted openers),
August 2026: the Crisis Daughter (buyer persona), a developmental editor, a
clinical reviewer (hospice/palliative + geriatrics lens), and a
privacy/sensitivity reviewer. Full findings summarized; consensus items
first.

## The verdict

**The spine works.** All four reviewers independently confirmed chapters
27, 28, and 29 as the heart of the book. The buyer persona: "Chapters 27,
28, and 29 are the spine of this book and they are already there... She
will cry at 'the sentence I am still learning to believe about myself.'"
The editor's protected list — lines no edit pass may touch:

- "We were going to drive on Friday. He didn't have a Friday."
- "Everyone knows that. / Everyone is wrong."
- "Grief and a checkbook do not belong in the same week."
- "This chapter is the meeting my family never had. Have it."
- "A DNR that exists in a drawer nobody can locate does not exist."
- "I put it on the cover so we would both have to look at it."

The work remaining is defensive: remove the handful of lines that hand the
reader her guilt back, fix one legal error, tune the hospice promises to
what hospice actually delivers, and close three privacy exposures.

## Consensus fixes (flagged by 2+ reviewers) — do these

### 1. Chapter 12 (Hospice) — overloaded, and the door is blocked
- **Buyer:** the reader opens this chapter terrified, and gets five
  paragraphs of the author's biography first. Start at or near "Then came
  Christmas Day, 2025." Compress sobriety/sponsorship to two sentences or
  move to the introduction.
- **Editor:** the chapter does three jobs at 443 words; target ~320. Cut
  the "measure this work in numbers" paragraph. Give the morphine-drip
  timeline back to Chapter 27 — it is 27's most devastating specific;
  Ch. 12 keeps only "His comfort was real. He did not suffer his way out
  of this world."
- **Clinical:** "I will show up every day until the last one" promises
  more than routine home hospice delivers (nurse 1–3×/week + 24/7 phone,
  daily only in crisis) — the exact expectations failure Ch. 11 warns
  about. Revise to "I am a phone call away every hour of every day and
  night, and I will keep showing up until the last day."
- **Clinical + privacy:** the drip-then-death-same-morning timeline needs
  one plain sentence so no reader infers causation: the drip starts
  *because* death is close, not the other way around. Also reword "His
  wife is a nurse... managed" so the care team owns the drip (protects
  the stepmom; see privacy).
- **Buyer:** add one line so Lou's good death reads as available to every
  family, not just ones with a medical insider: that skill is what a
  hospice team brings into every home.

### 2. Chapter 20 (Healthcare POA) — one legal error + one excluded reader
- **Clinical (accuracy):** "excluded from their mother's healthcare
  decisions" is wrong as a general rule — most states have next-of-kin
  surrogate statutes. What's lost without a POA is clarity, speed, and
  standing when family disagrees. Revise per the clinical review's
  suggested language.
- **Clinical (safety) + buyer:** add the two counterweights — (a) a
  dementia diagnosis is NOT incapacity; many people in early stages can
  still legally sign; an elder-law attorney can assess ("go this week");
  (b) one sentence of rope for the reader whose window already closed:
  "missing a window nobody showed you is not the same as failing. This
  chapter covers both."
- **Editor:** cut the closing aphorism ("Today is early...") — end on the
  folder.

### 3. Chapter 24 (Advance Care Planning) — contradicts 28, pre-spends 27
- **All three content reviewers:** "The last days are too late for
  meaningful conversations" contradicts Ch. 28 ("say the things — none of
  it is wasted") and wounds the reader at a bedside right now. Fix: "too
  late for the practical conversations — and never too late for the
  loving ones," plus a pointer to Ch. 28.
- **Editor:** the "two things give me peace" material pre-resolves
  Chapter 27's engine. Move said-everything/last-memory to 27; Ch. 24
  keeps the practical-unsaid lesson and one line: "I was not in the room
  when my dad died. That story belongs to Chapter 27." Target ~280 words.
- **Buyer:** cut the *Cardinal's Promise* plug — reads as a sales pitch at
  11pm; keep "don't leave anything unsaid," move the book mention to back
  matter.

### 4. Chapter 27 (Last Days Plan) — the backlash paragraph is identifiable
- **Privacy:** naming John, Joel, Chris, and Lisa at the bedside makes the
  unattributed backlash paragraph attributable by elimination — and casts
  suspicion on siblings who may have said nothing. Cheapest fix, loses
  nothing: de-name the bedside ("my brothers and sister, my stepmom") and
  change "My brother had been calling" to "My brothers had been calling."
  Alternative: keep names and have each sibling read Ch. 27 and consent.
- **Privacy (tone):** "the score was never real" adjudicates the family
  dispute in print; consider "There was never a score" (editor's version)
  or "the score was never mine to carry."
- **Editor:** 724 words; target ~550. Cut the first "reason this book has
  the title" (keep the second), tighten the honesty paragraph.

### 5. Chapter 3 (Dementia) — take the words seriously for one beat
- **Clinical (safety):** before the therapy resolution, add the
  taken-seriously beat: alarming words from a person with dementia get
  the gentle questions every time, AND a look for the other signs
  (unexplained bruises, fear of one face, sudden change), AND the
  ombudsman/administrator if answers don't add up. "That day, every
  answer pointed to the therapy room. That's the only reason the story
  ends gently."
- **Privacy:** do NOT add Nana's verbatim crude quotes (open item) —
  paraphrase carries the teaching; her daughter would carry the quote.

### 6. Chapter 10 (Skilled Nursing) — Marion and Frank exposure
- **Privacy (HIGH):** first names + "Gulf Breeze Hospital" + broken hip +
  ongoing recovery = identifiable in that community, with grim prognosis
  statistics printed at a living man. Required: written consent after
  they read the passage, OR true pseudonyms + "the local hospital" +
  blurred relationship. Freeze the ending before typeset; re-consent if
  it changes.
- **Buyer:** close the "she doesn't have a Rob to call" loop — add "You
  don't have a friend in the industry. This chapter is that phone call."

## Single-reviewer fixes worth making

- **Ch. 1 (buyer):** seed the title promise on page one — one line after
  "This chapter is for that moment": whatever you think you should have
  seen coming — it's not your fault; there were no symptoms we'd missed
  either.
- **Ch. 2 (buyer + editor):** defuse "families who learn to read them" (a
  competence test aimed at the reader) — "Nobody catches everything — I
  didn't, and this is my profession." Editor's tighter line: "They are
  information. Read them early enough, and you get to make decisions
  instead of reacting."
- **Ch. 17 (clinical):** scope the income-only rule to manufacturer/
  charity programs; Medicaid asks hardest about assets (look-back, estate
  recovery) — "for that one, get a guide before you file." Keep "let them
  say no."
- **Ch. 19 (clinical):** PDMP claim is overstated (covers controlled
  substances; is checked, doesn't auto-flag), and the missing net is the
  pharmacist: "fill every prescription at one pharmacy, so one computer
  sees every bottle." Soften "Doctors... don't communicate" to "You
  cannot count on..."
- **Ch. 22 (clinical):** "the state steps in" → a court and a judge step
  in (often appointing a family member, under oversight); add "execution
  requirements vary by state." **Editor:** "everything waits. So does the
  person it's all for."
- **Ch. 28 (clinical):** add the escalation path — "call the hospice
  24-hour line, not 911; that's what it's for" (scoped to expected deaths
  on hospice); "restlessness that looks like distress is treatable — call
  the team"; hearing line → "No one can prove what reaches them — so we
  assume it all does. Say the things." Offering food for pleasure is
  still allowed — "offer, never force." **Editor:** "It is not death. It
  is *the* not knowing." Cut "arriving on purpose."
- **Ch. 21 (editor):** cut "It replaces guessing with knowing" (filler);
  compress the findability paragraph to one sentence pointing at Ch. 23.
- **Ch. 8 notes (clinical):** disclose the locator incentive in the same
  breath as the recommendation — free because the facility pays them,
  which also means they only show contracted facilities.
- **Ch. 13 notes (clinical):** don't print the blanket "straight Medicare
  + secondary is best" verdict — Medigap underwriting and cost cut both
  ways; let the neutral-party advice (SHIP counselors) carry the chapter.

## Voice pass (editor) — one sweep across all openers

- **The closer tic:** "This chapter is about/for/shows you" ends 12 of 16
  openers. Keep it where the sentence is the punch (Chs. 1, 5, 4's
  planned closer); cut or vary at least six (2, 9, 12, 17, 24, 29).
- **Kill the decorative triads** (Ch. 12's "comfort instead of pain.
  Dignity instead of machines. Presence instead of fear." is the loudest
  AI tell in the set) — but protect the creed triad ("I will not let you
  be in pain...").
- **De-stack aphorisms:** one per opener, max. Cut the second of any pair
  (Chs. 5, 20, 21, 24).
- **Em dashes:** Ch. 27 has 15; several 250-word openers run one per 3–4
  sentences. Replace appositive dashes with periods. No semicolons —
  nobody speaks a semicolon.
- **Self-ranking:** five "…in this book" superlatives; keep one (Ch. 19's).
- **Top line edits:** the editor's ranked eight, including "no forgiveness
  withheld, nothing I was still saving up to say" (Ch. 24) and "The
  disease took her memory. It took her manners too." (Ch. 3).
- **Duplicate beat:** "the one thing that week was for: grieving" appears
  nearly verbatim in Chs. 24 and 29 — Ch. 29 owns it.
- **Continuity:** say in Ch. 2 whether the 2023 tumor was the 2015 cancer
  returned or new. Reorder the file so Ch. 28 precedes Ch. 29.
