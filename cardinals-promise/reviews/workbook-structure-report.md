# Worksheet Placement — Structural Consultant Report

Commissioned September 2026 to answer: should the fill-in worksheets be
pulled out of the chapters into a workbook at the back of the book?

## The finding that reframes the question

The worksheets are **already consolidated.** All 29 chapters share one
rigid anatomy:

    opener → plain (Plain Truth) → think (What Families Usually Think)
    → [alert] → teaching → week (What to Do This Week)
    → WORKSHEET(S) → [script] → rob (Rob's Note) → takeaway

All 55 worksheets sit in that single slot — after "What to Do This
Week," before "Rob's Note." **None interrupts teaching prose.** The
question is therefore not "consolidate scattered worksheets" but "move
29 small end-of-chapter workbooks into one large back one." The gain is
already banked.

## Recommendation: keep them inline; add 2-3 pages of back matter

1. **Worksheet Finder** (1 page, back matter) — all 55 tools by name,
   grouped by part, with chapter and page, each carrying its chapter's
   colored dot. Delivers the only real benefit of a back workbook
   (findability) for one page instead of sixty.
2. **Refrigerator Page** (1-2 pages, facing *Numbers That Matter*) — NOT
   a copy of an existing worksheet. A new distilled merge of what must
   live outside the book: emergency contacts, current medications and
   pharmacy, where the documents are and what medical orders exist.
   Styled neutral gray (not an urgency color), with a disclaimer echoing
   Ch. 23's. Closes the expert panel's open "one master contacts page" ask.
3. **Refill line** — one QR + short URL on the Finder page and one
   sentence on the copyright page's existing photocopy grant.
4. **One sentence in "How to Use This Handbook"** pointing to both.

## Why inline wins

- **The worksheet is the chapter's action beat.** Sequence runs story →
  truth → teaching → do this → the tool → Rob's heart → the one
  question. Remove the tool and Rob's Note and the Takeaway land
  back-to-back with nothing for her hands. Ch. 2's Fall Log is printed
  twelve lines from Rob's Note asking her to keep one.
- **19 of the 55 have no fill lines at all** — the "Questions to Ask"
  scripts (21 boxes, 14 with zero blanks). The expert panel called the
  scripts the book's highest-value content. Reference text belongs
  beside its topic; any migration is partial from the start.
- **Ch. 23 is a documented safety case.** A family taped the filled
  preferences worksheet to the fridge believing it was a DNR. Pooling it
  with 54 official-looking forms amplifies that failure mode. Stays
  inline permanently.
- **Instruments cannot leave their keys.** Ch. 4's Burnout Self-Check is
  scored (/32); Ch. 16's Policy Finder is a six-step procedure; Ch. 13's
  worksheet restates the chapter's central teaching as a decision aid.
- **Color amputation.** All 55 inherit `var(--c)` from the chapter
  section. Relocation needs 55 manual re-tags, and a mixed-color back
  section violates the locked rule that urgency colors are load-bearing,
  never decorative — roughly 30% of the book's surface goes color-mute.

## Category evidence

- *The 36-Hour Day* and *How to Care for Aging Parents* keep checklists
  and question lists inside topic chapters; the back is a resource
  directory. *Being Mortal* has no worksheets at all.
- The worksheet-forward products (*Get It Together*, *The Big Book of
  Everything*) are organizers where the form IS the book.
- **No successful title in this category is built "teaching in front,
  forms in back."** The pattern that works commercially is a separate
  SKU (*Boundaries* + workbook) — which Rob already has planned as the
  NC companion.
- **On completion rates, be honest:** there is no published controlled
  study of inline vs. back-of-book exercise completion in trade
  nonfiction. Anyone quoting a percentage is inventing it. What is
  established: the split-attention effect (Sweller & Chandler), the
  spatial contiguity principle (Mayer), and navigation friction — all
  pointing the same direction, and worst under cognitive load, which is
  this reader's defining condition.

## Print reality

- **Page count goes UP** (+8 to +15): lost page-sharing, ~29 new heads,
  new divider and mini-TOC, back-reference lines. More per-unit premium
  color cost on every copy, forever.
- **Spine grows** ~0.02-0.035" → new cover wrap from a designer already
  mid-flight on print-res art.
- **Perfect binding opens worst at the first and last thirds.**
  Concentrating all 55 writing tasks in the final 60 pages puts every
  one in the hardest-opening part of the block. Inline is physically
  easier to write in. KDP offers no spiral/lay-flat option at all.
- **Index rework is the hidden cost**: ~200 hand-set page references
  with ranges and bolded primaries, plus 39 hard-coded TOC spans.
- **EPUB**: fill rules are hostile to reflow wherever they live (already
  fixed, position-independent). Inline is the *better* ebook structure —
  in a back workbook every worksheet becomes an internal-link round trip.
- **Audio**: inline maps to the ACX companion PDF in chapter order,
  which is how the listener thinks.

## The crisis reader

The book's entire navigation is single-address: the triage table, the
Red/Purple/Blue spread, five pathways, and 198 in-body "Chapter N"
references all say *go to Chapter N*. A back workbook makes every crisis
route two-address. At 11pm in a hospital corridor, the second address is
where she stops.

**And a color rule would run backwards from intuition:** RED chapters
(2, 4, 10, 12, 23, 26, 27, 28) must keep their tools — the worksheet is
the instrument of the act. BLUE chapters are where back placement is
tolerable. So a color rule would move the *blue* worksheets, leaving the
system half-amputated.

Important nuance: **color marks when a page is USED, not when it's
FILLED.** Ch. 26 is red because you use it in an emergency, but you fill
it on a quiet Tuesday. That gap is what the Refrigerator Page closes —
the finished artifact belongs outside the book entirely.

## If a hybrid anyway — the rule, and the only four that qualify

> A worksheet earns a back-of-book home only if **the finished page's
> job happens outside the book.** After she fills it in, where does the
> paper go? Back into the book → stays inline. Onto the refrigerator,
> into a go-bag, into an EMT's hand → tear-out. All four must hold:
> living record; read by someone else; chapter-independent; needed when
> the book is not open to that chapter.

| Ch. | Worksheet | Note |
|---|---|---|
| 25 | Medication and Doctor List | App panel called it the highest-value single request |
| 26 | Emergency Contacts & Crisis Plan | Filled calm, used frantic |
| 19 | Documents Folder Checklist | Weakest of the four; drop first if pages are tight |
| 27 | Last Days Plan | Author's call; move only as a DUPLICATE, never a removal |

**Duplicate, never relocate** — keep the full version in the chapter and
add a blank full-page tear-out in the back (~7-9 pages). Relocating
reintroduces two-address navigation for four crisis-critical chapters.

## The lead magnet

Supplement: yes. Replacement: no.

- The copyright page already grants photocopying for family use; a
  print-ready PDF just makes that frictionless, and solves the one real
  problem inline worksheets have — **you only get one copy** (a daughter
  touring four communities needs the Ch. 8 sheet four times).
- **Gating is the specific 1-star trigger in this category**: "the
  worksheets aren't in the book, you have to give them your email." The
  Amazon listing promises the opposite in plain language.
- She has no laptop or printer at 11pm. Gated content is content she
  does not have on the night she needs it.
- Link rot outlives the funnel; the book circulates for a decade.
- A meaningful share of readers are 70+ spouses. Print-only is a feature.
- A complete free pack also undercuts the paid NC companion.

Structure: book complete with nothing but a pen; ONE back-matter page
with QR + short URL framed as refills; one line on the copyright page;
no email wall on the worksheets (offer the list separately on the same
page); one address, not 29 in-chapter URLs.

## Migration risks if overruled

Critical: Ch. 23 safety regression. High: color amputation (55 re-tags),
index rework (~200 refs), navigation doubling (198 cross-refs become
two-address, a permanent maintenance burden). Medium: page/spine/cover
regen; openers churn (12 of 29 still open, Ch. 10 blocked on consent);
loss of the action beat. Strategic: cannibalizes the NC companion.

## Timing

Do not restructure while 12 chapter openers are still open and Ch. 10 is
blocked on written consent. Revisit only with real data — Amazon reviews
and the beta form. If readers report "I couldn't find the form again,"
the fix is a better Finder or an index tab-bleed, not relocation.

**One-sentence version:** the worksheets are already consolidated at the
end of every chapter in that chapter's urgency color, the book's crisis
navigation promises one address, so keep them there, add a Worksheet
Finder and a Refrigerator Page — and let the consolidated workbook be
the North Carolina companion you're already selling.
