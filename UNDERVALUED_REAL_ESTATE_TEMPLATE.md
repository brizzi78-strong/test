# Step-by-Step Template: Finding Cheap & Undervalued Real Estate

A repeatable, fill-in-the-blanks system for sourcing, screening, valuing, and closing on below-market properties. Work through the steps in order; copy the worksheets for each new market or deal.

> **Disclaimer:** This is an educational template, not financial, legal, or tax advice. Verify everything locally and consult licensed professionals before purchasing.

## Step 2 — Pick a Market Where Cheap ≠ Bad

Cheap properties in dying markets aren't undervalued — they're correctly priced. Score each candidate market (1–5 on each line):

- [ ] **Population trend:** growing or stable over the last 10 years (Census data)
- [ ] **Job market:** diverse employers, unemployment at or below national average
- [ ] **Rent-to-price ratio:** monthly rent ≥ ~0.8–1% of purchase price is a strong sign
- [ ] **Days on market (DOM):** rising DOM = more negotiating leverage
- [ ] **Landlord/permit climate:** check local landlord-tenant rules and property tax trends
- [ ] **Path of progress:** new infrastructure, hospitals, universities, distribution centers

**Free data sources:** Census.gov, Bureau of Labor Statistics, Zillow Research, Redfin Data Center, city planning department websites, local Facebook groups and subreddits.

**Target:** Markets scoring 20+ out of 30. Pick 1–2 markets max and go deep, not wide.

## Step 3 — Build Your Deal-Flow Pipeline (Where Cheap Deals Actually Hide)

Undervalued deals are rarely sitting on page 1 of Zillow. Set up ALL of the following channels and check them on the listed cadence:

### On-Market (daily, 15 min)
- [ ] Saved searches on Zillow/Redfin/Realtor.com filtered to your buy box, sorted by **newest** and by **price cuts**
- [ ] Keyword searches in listings: *"as-is," "TLC," "handyman special," "estate sale," "motivated," "bring all offers," "cash only," "sold as is"*
- [ ] Listings with **90+ days on market** — sellers are worn down and negotiable
- [ ] Expired/withdrawn listings (via an agent with MLS access)

### Distressed & Legal Notices (weekly)
- [ ] County **pre-foreclosure / lis pendens** filings (county recorder website)
- [ ] **Tax-delinquent** property lists (county treasurer — often free or a few dollars)
- [ ] **Probate** filings (county court records) — heirs often want fast, simple sales
- [ ] **Code violation** lists (city code enforcement) — tired landlords and absentee owners
- [ ] Auction sites: Auction.com, Hubzu, Xome, local sheriff's sales
- [ ] HUD Homes (hudhomestore.gov) and bank REO lists

### Off-Market Outreach (weekly)
- [ ] **Driving for dollars:** log vacant/neglected houses (boarded windows, tall grass, full mailboxes), then look up owners via the county assessor
- [ ] **Direct mail** to absentee owners, tax-delinquent owners, and inherited properties (lists from PropStream, ListSource, or DIY from county data)
- [ ] **Networking:** wholesalers, property managers, estate attorneys, local REIA meetings — tell everyone your exact buy box
- [ ] FSBO sites, Craigslist, Facebook Marketplace ("for sale by owner")

**Weekly pipeline goal:** ______ new leads reviewed | ______ analyzed | ______ offers made
*(A common baseline: 100 reviewed → 10 analyzed → 3 offers → 1 accepted. It's a numbers game.)*

## Step 4 — Screen Fast: The 5-Minute Filter

Don't deep-dive every lead. Run this filter and discard anything that fails two or more:

1. **Price vs. area:** Is asking price at least 15–20% below the median for comparable homes in that zip?
2. **Why is it cheap?** *Fixable* reasons (ugly, dated, vacant, bad photos, estate, tired landlord) = proceed. *Unfixable* reasons (busy road, flood zone, foundation on a cliff, terrible school zone, functional obsolescence) = pass.
3. **Rent check:** Does estimated rent (Rentometer / Zillow rent estimate / local PM quote) hit your ratio target?
4. **Structural red flags in photos:** roof sag, stair-step foundation cracks, water staining, fire damage — price in a bigger rehab or pass.
5. **Days on market / motivation signals:** price cuts, "seller relocating," vacant, tenant issues.

Leads that pass go into the deal analyzer (Step 5).

## Step 5 — Run the Numbers (The Deal Analyzer)

### 5a. Establish ARV (After-Repair Value) with Comps
Pull 3–6 **sold** (not listed) comps that are:
- Within ~0.5 miles (1 mile rural)
- Sold in the last 3–6 months
- Within ±20% of square footage, same bed/bath count and property type

| 3. ________ | $______ | ____ | $____ | ____ | ____ |

**ARV = median $/sq ft of comps × subject sq ft = $________**
*(Use the median, not the best comp. Be pessimistic.)*

### 5b. Estimate Rehab
Quick rule-of-thumb tiers (adjust for your market):
- Cosmetic (paint, floors, fixtures): ~$15–30/sq ft
- Moderate (kitchen, baths, some systems): ~$30–60/sq ft
- Gut/heavy (roof, HVAC, plumbing, electrical, structure): ~$60–100+/sq ft

**Rehab estimate: $________ + 15% contingency = $________**

### 5c. Apply Your Deal Test

**For flips — the 70% Rule:**
```
Max Offer = (ARV × 0.70) − Rehab Costs
Max Offer = ($________ × 0.70) − $________ = $________
```

**For rentals — cash flow test:**
```
Monthly rent:                     $________
− Taxes, insurance:               $________
− Vacancy (8%):                   $________
− Maintenance + CapEx (10–15%):   $________
− Property management (8–10%):    $________
− Mortgage payment:               $________
= Monthly cash flow:              $________   (target: ≥ $150–200/door)

Cap rate = Annual NOI ÷ All-in cost = ______%  (know your market's norm)
Cash-on-cash = Annual cash flow ÷ Cash invested = ______%  (target: ≥ 8–10%)
```

**A property is "undervalued" only if the numbers say so at the price you can actually get it for — not because it feels cheap.**

## Step 6 — Verify Before You Commit (Due Diligence Checklist)

- [ ] Walk the property (or send a local agent/inspector with video)
- [ ] Professional inspection — never waive it on a distressed property
- [ ] Title search: liens, back taxes, code fines, HOA arrears (all common on cheap properties, and they convey to you)
- [ ] Sewer scope + roof, HVAC, electrical panel, water heater ages
- [ ] Flood zone / insurance quote **before** closing (insurance can kill a thin deal)
- [ ] Confirm real property taxes **after** reassessment at your purchase price
- [ ] If tenant-occupied: leases, rent roll, security deposits, payment history, estoppel
- [ ] Permit history — unpermitted additions can subtract value, not add it
- [ ] Re-verify rent estimate with an actual local property manager

**Deal-breaker found?** Renegotiate with documented repair bids, or walk. Sunk time is not a reason to buy.

---

## Step 7 — Make Offers That Get Cheap Prices Accepted

- **Anchor to the numbers:** Offer your calculated max minus a negotiation buffer (5–10%). Attach your reasoning (repair bids, comps) — it makes low offers credible instead of insulting.
- **Trade speed and certainty for price:** cash or hard-money proof of funds, short inspection window, flexible closing date, buying as-is (with inspection for information, not renegotiation).
- **Solve the seller's actual problem:** estate sellers want *done*, landlords want *out*, pre-foreclosure sellers want *debt relief before the auction date*. Ask "what matters most to you — price or timing?"
- **Follow up relentlessly:** most below-market deals close on the 2nd–5th contact, after a listing expires or another buyer falls through. Calendar a follow-up every 2–3 weeks on every "no."
- **Never exceed your max number.** Losing a deal costs nothing; overpaying costs for years.

## Quick-Reference: Red Flags vs. Green Flags on "Cheap" Listings

| 🟢 Fixable discount (buy) | 🔴 Permanent discount (pass) |
|---|---|
| Ugly finishes, dated kitchen | Adjacent to highway, industrial, or power lines |
| Bad listing photos / vacant | Chronic flooding or uninsurable |
| Estate sale, tired landlord | Severely declining area with no employers |
| Deferred cosmetic maintenance | Major structural issues priced beyond ARV support |
| Mispriced by out-of-area agent | Functional obsolescence (no bath on bedroom floor, 1-bed layout in family market) |
| Long DOM in a slow season | Title problems the seller can't cure |

## 30-Day Kickstart Plan

- **Week 1:** Complete Steps 1–2 (buy box + market). Set up all saved searches.
- **Week 2:** Build Step 3 pipeline — pull the tax-delinquent list, join a local REIA, contact 2 agents and 2 wholesalers with your buy box.
- **Week 3:** Analyze 10 properties end-to-end with Step 5. Speed comes from reps.
- **Week 4:** Make your first 3 offers at your numbers. Log everything in Step 8.

Repeat weekly. Consistency in Steps 3–5 is the entire game.
