A promise you can check.

HOP is the Hope coin, named in honor of the founder’s wife. It belongs to the same family story as The Cardinal’s Promise, and it is built the way a promise should be: said out loud, in public, where anyone can check it. Everything official about HOP lives on this site, and nowhere else.

Think of it as a souvenir, not an investment. A keepsake with a promise attached, recorded somewhere anyone can check that the promise was kept. Nobody should buy one expecting to make money, and we will never suggest otherwise. Please don't spend money you can't live without — use money you'd be at peace never seeing again.

It has not launched yet. There is no HOP token on the blockchain today. Nothing is for sale. When that changes, you will read it here first.

Watch out for fakes. Until launch day, any token calling itself HOP is not ours. Scammers copy names. The one and only official contract address will ever live in one place: this page. If it isn't posted here, it isn't ours.

The book, and the coins that come with it

The coin and the book belong to the same promise, so they come together: every copy of The Cardinal's Promise bought directly from the book's site comes with a small, fixed number of HOP — a keepsake of the story, not a rebate. The exact number will be announced at launch, coins will be claimable here, and the program's wallet will be published on the ledger so anyone can watch it.

Please don't buy the book for the coins. They are worth almost nothing in money and may always be. Buy the book if you want the book. Copies bought through retailers can't include coins — only direct purchases can.

If you want to help someone, don't buy a coin

We want to be blunt about this, because most projects like this are not. Buying a souvenir is not a donation. If what you want is to actually help someone, give money directly to the people already doing that work. It arrives whole, it arrives faster, and none of it passes through us.

What the code does

These aren't things we're asking you to believe. They're built into the contract, and on launch day you'll be able to check every one of them yourself.

  • A fixed supply. 250,000,000 HOP. No more can ever be created. The code does not contain a way to do it.
  • No special powers. No pause switch. No blacklist. No trap that stops you selling. And on launch day, ownership of the contract is given up permanently — after that, no one can change the rules. Not even us.
  • 40% goes into the public pool. 100,000,000 HOP enters the public trading pool at launch.
  • The pool is locked. The liquidity behind the coin is locked for 12 months with a public lock anyone can inspect. We could not pull it out if we wanted to.
  • 40% is the founder's, and it is not locked. 100,000,000 HOP is held by the founder in an ordinary wallet. It is the largest single holding, it is roughly the same size as the entire trading pool, and there is no code stopping it from being sold. We considered locking it and chose not to. You should weigh that against everything else on this page, and you should not need us to tell you what it means. The address is published on the ledger page so you can watch it yourself, and we've committed in writing to announcing any sale before it happens — but that is a promise, not a lock, and you should treat the two differently.
  • 20% is visible. 50,000,000 HOP sits in a treasury wallet named on this page, behind a multi-signature wallet that needs more than one person to move anything. Anyone can watch that wallet, at any time, without asking us anything.

What it costs to buy one

Every trade costs 0.3%. That's a fee, and we're not going to call it anything softer. It's charged by Uniswap on every coin traded there, not something we added to this one, and it stays in the locked pool rather than going to a wallet we can reach into.

There is no fee that only sellers pay, and none that gets steeper if you leave early. Those exist elsewhere. They're how people get trapped, and we'd rather you be able to walk away at any hour of any day without a penalty for changing your mind.

The bigger cost is usually the network, not us. Ethereum charges a few dollars to process any transaction, and it's a flat amount rather than a percentage — so a very small purchase loses a large share of itself to it. Worth knowing before you spend $20 and watch a quarter of it disappear.

And the pool is small. At launch there are only a few thousand dollars behind this coin, which means even a modest buy moves the price a lot, and so does a modest sell. That is not a feature. It is what a coin this size actually looks like, and you should know it before you spend anything.

The plainest risk here, said once, plainly. The founder holds 100,000,000 HOP, unlocked, against a pool holding a few thousand dollars. If he sold even a fraction of it, the price would collapse. Nothing in the code prevents that. What exists instead is a published address you can watch, and a written commitment to announce any sale in advance. If that isn't enough for you, it shouldn't be — don't buy.

What the treasury is, honestly

50,000,000 HOP — 20% of everything — sits in a wallet published on this page. We're not going to tell you a percentage of it goes to charity, because we don't know yet and we'd rather not name a number we'd have to walk back.

Some of it has to cover the real costs of doing this properly: the liquidity lock, network fees, legal, accounting, the work itself. What remains, we'd like to give away — many small gifts to many families and small organizations, chosen by a giving committee and posted here with receipts, not one large check that makes a headline.

It is also worth saying plainly what the treasury is not: it is coins, not money. Turning any of it into dollars would mean selling into the same small pool everyone else trades in. So do not read the treasury as a budget we are sitting on. Nothing gets spent from it quietly, and anything that does gets posted here first.

That's an intention, not a promise, and the difference matters. What is certain is that you can watch the wallet. Every coin that leaves it is visible to anyone, forever, without asking us anything — and we'll post gifts after they're made, with a link to the transaction. Receipts, not pledges.
