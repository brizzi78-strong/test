Built to hold.

MAGR is the Mager Coin. It is named for Jeff Mager, who finished a 140.6-mile triathlon — swim, bike, then a marathon — and the name is meant the way that distance is meant: nothing about it is quick, and the only way through is to keep going. The coin is built the same way. Slowly, in public, where anyone can check the work.

Think of it as a souvenir, not an investment. "Built to hold" is about how this was made — fixed supply, locked liquidity, no way for us to change the rules later. It is not advice to buy one and wait for a price. Nobody should buy expecting to make money, and we will never suggest otherwise. Please don't spend money you can't live without — use money you'd be at peace never seeing again.

It has not launched yet. There is no MAGR token on the blockchain today. Nothing is for sale. When that changes, you will read it here first.

Watch out for fakes. Until launch day, any token calling itself MAGR or Mager Coin is not ours. Scammers copy names. The one and only official contract address will ever live in one place: this page. If it isn't posted here, it isn't ours.

If you want to help, don't buy a coin

We want to be blunt about this, because most projects like this are not. Buying a souvenir is not a donation. If what you want is to actually help someone, give money directly to the people already doing that work. It arrives whole, it arrives faster, and none of it passes through us.

These are the places that carried this family. None has any connection to this coin, and none has endorsed it — they simply did the work, and we'd rather point you at them than at ourselves.

  • Perdido Bay Baptist Church Lou's church, on Sorrento Road. Of everything in his life, this is the one he cared about.
  • Lakeview Center Pensacola. Treatment for addiction and mental illness. There is a life here to write about because of work like theirs.
  • The Napoleon Hill Foundation Teaching people they're capable of more than they were told.

What the code does

These aren't things we're asking you to believe. They're built into the contract, and on launch day you'll be able to check every one of them yourself.

  • A fixed supply. 250,000,000 MAGR. No more can ever be created. The code does not contain a way to do it.
  • No special powers. No pause switch. No blacklist. No trap that stops you selling. And on launch day, ownership of the contract is given up permanently — after that, no one can change the rules. Not even us.
  • 40% goes into the public pool. 100,000,000 MAGR enters the public trading pool at launch.
  • The pool is locked. The liquidity behind the coin is locked for 12 months with a public lock anyone can inspect. We could not pull it out if we wanted to.
  • 40% is the founder's, and it is locked too. 100,000,000 MAGR is held by the founder in a timelock contract with a published release schedule. It is the largest single holding, we are not going to pretend otherwise, and the lock is the reason you don't have to take our word about it. The address and the unlock dates are on the ledger page.
  • 20% is visible. 50,000,000 MAGR sits in a treasury wallet named on this page, behind a multi-signature wallet that needs more than one person to move anything. Anyone can watch that wallet, at any time, without asking us anything.

What it costs to buy one

Every trade costs 0.3%. That's a fee, and we're not going to call it anything softer. It's charged by Uniswap on every coin traded there, not something we added to this one, and it stays in the locked pool rather than going to a wallet we can reach into.

There is no fee that only sellers pay, and none that gets steeper if you leave early. Those exist elsewhere. They're how people get trapped, and we'd rather you be able to walk away at any hour of any day without a penalty for changing your mind.

The bigger cost is usually the network, not us. Ethereum charges a few dollars to process any transaction, and it's a flat amount rather than a percentage — so a very small purchase loses a large share of itself to it. Worth knowing before you spend $20 and watch a quarter of it disappear.

And the pool is small. At launch there are only a few thousand dollars behind this coin, which means even a modest buy moves the price a lot, and so does a modest sell. That is not a feature. It is what a coin this size actually looks like, and you should know it before you spend anything.

What the treasury is, honestly

50,000,000 MAGR — 20% of everything — sits in a wallet published on this page. We're not going to tell you a percentage of it goes to charity, because we don't know yet and we'd rather not name a number we'd have to walk back.

Some of it has to cover the real costs of doing this properly: the liquidity lock, network fees, legal, accounting, the work itself. What remains, we'd like to give — to the kinds of places listed above, and given widely rather than in one place. Many small gifts to many families and small organizations, not one large check that makes a headline.

It is also worth saying plainly what the treasury is not: it is coins, not money. Turning any of it into dollars would mean selling into the same small pool everyone else trades in. So do not read the treasury as a budget we are sitting on. Nothing gets spent from it quietly, and anything that does gets posted here first.

That's an intention, not a promise, and the difference matters. What is certain is that you can watch the wallet. Every coin that leaves it is visible to anyone, forever, without asking us anything — and we'll post gifts after they're made, with a link to the transaction. Receipts, not pledges.
