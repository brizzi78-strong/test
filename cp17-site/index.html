The Cardinals Promise coin

A promise you can check.

CARD is the Cardinals Promise coin. It carries the name of a promise made in this family, and it is built the way a promise should be: said out loud, in public, where anyone can hold you to it.

Think of it as a souvenir, not an investment. A keepsake with a promise attached, recorded somewhere anyone can check that the promise was kept. Nobody should buy one expecting to make money, and we will never suggest otherwise. Please don't spend money you can't live without — use money you'd be at peace never seeing again.

Why this coin exists

Lou Brizzi was born in 1941. Into his eighties he stayed curious about technology, and — of all things — about cryptocurrency. He kept reading about it, kept asking about it, kept looking forward at an age when most people have stopped looking. He died in 2025.

CARD starts there. It is not a business idea that went looking for a story to attach itself to afterward. It is his curiosity, carried forward by his family into something that could actually be built.

This is an extension of my dad's story. I don't have to make anything up.

Rob Brizzi

That is also why this page reads the way it does. A coin made out of somebody's memory doesn't get to be vague about what it is. What the code is intended to do

The contract is written and tested but not yet deployed, so today these are intentions. On launch day each one becomes a thing you can check yourself, and we will replace every line below with a proof link as it happens. If a line never gets its proof link, treat it as untrue.

  • A fixed supply. 1,000,000,000 CARD. No more can ever be created. The code does not contain a way to do it.
  • No special powers. No pause switch. No blacklist. No trap that stops you selling. And on launch day, ownership of the contract is given up permanently — after that, no one can change the rules. Not even us.
  • A 2% fee, fixed forever. On every trade between ordinary wallets, 2% of the CARD goes to the treasury wallet and the other side gets 98%. It applies to buys and sells equally. It does not apply to coins sent to or from the treasury itself, so a gift to the treasury arrives whole. The rate and the destination are written into the contract. There is no switch. No one can raise it, lower it, turn it off, or point it somewhere else — not even us, not ever.
  • 40% goes into the public pool. 400,000,000 CARD enters the public trading pool at launch, and the pool is locked for 12 months with a public lock anyone can inspect. We could not pull it out if we wanted to.
  • 40% is the founder's, and it is not locked. 400,000,000 CARD is held by the founder in an ordinary wallet. It is the largest single holding, it is roughly the same size as the entire trading pool, and there is no code stopping it from being sold. We considered locking it and chose not to. You should weigh that against everything else on this page. The address is published on the ledger page so you can watch it yourself, and we've committed in writing to announcing any sale before it happens — but that is a promise, not a lock, and you should treat the two differently.
  • 20% is visible. 200,000,000 CARD sits in the treasury wallet above, 0xDAE63eBEe60A691e1538D480AE3F6509068ab300. Every trade also sends 2% of the coins traded to this wallet. That is the treasury's income and the project's only revenue. Today it is a single wallet held by the founder — one key, not a multi-signature wallet, and you should read it that way. The fee's destination is baked into the contract, so even if the keys move, the fee keeps flowing to this same address forever.

What it will cost to buy one

Every trade costs 2%. That's a fee, and we're not going to call it anything softer. It is built into the coin, it goes to the treasury wallet, and it applies in both directions: when you buy, 2% of the CARD the pool sends you goes to the treasury and you get 98%; when you sell, 2% of the CARD you send goes to the treasury and the pool prices the other 98%. It is the only money this project makes.

On top of that, Uniswap charges its own 0.3% each way. That one isn't ours, and it stays in the locked pool rather than going to a wallet we can reach into. Put together, a round trip — buy, then sell — costs about 4.5% before network gas, slippage, and price impact. We would rather you read that number here than discover it in your wallet.

There is no fee that only sellers pay, and none that gets steeper if you leave early. The 2% is flat and the same in both directions. The other kinds exist elsewhere. They're how people get trapped, and we'd rather you be able to walk away at any hour of any day without a penalty for changing your mind.

The bigger cost is usually the network, not us. Ethereum charges a few dollars to process any transaction, and it's a flat amount rather than a percentage — so a very small purchase loses a large share of itself to it. Worth knowing before you spend $20 and watch a quarter of it disappear.

And the pool is small. At launch there are only a few thousand dollars behind this coin, which means even a modest buy moves the price a lot, and so does a modest sell. That is not a feature. It is what a coin this size actually looks like, and you should know it before you spend anything.

The plainest risk here, said once, plainly. The founder holds 400,000,000 CARD, unlocked, against a pool holding a few thousand dollars. If he sold even a fraction of it, the price would collapse. Nothing in the code prevents that. What exists instead is a published address you can watch, and a written commitment to announce any sale in advance. If that isn't enough for you, it shouldn't be — don't buy.

No proof, no claim. We will not call liquidity locked, ownership renounced, source verified, or trading live until the corresponding on-chain evidence is linked from this site.

What the treasury is, honestly

The treasury is not a charitable donation account and buying CARD is not a donation. The treasury / launch wallet is now public so anyone can monitor its activity: 0xDAE63eBEe60A691e1538D480AE3F6509068ab300.

200,000,000 CARD — 20% of everything — will sit there at launch, and every trade adds 2% of the coins traded. That is the treasury's income and the project's only revenue. Honestly, at 2% it might cover the small fixed costs — the LLC, the website — if people trade. If they don't, it covers nothing. It does not fund lawyers or accountants.

It is also coins, not money. Turning any of it into dollars would mean selling into the same small pool everyone else trades in. So do not read the treasury as a budget we are sitting on. Nothing gets spent from it quietly, and anything that does gets posted here first.

What remains after real costs, we'd like to give — to the kinds of places listed above, and given widely rather than in one place. That's an intention, not a promise. Any charitable giving remains an intention unless and until a completed gift and its evidence are published. Receipts, not pledges.
