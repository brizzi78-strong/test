A promise you can check.

CARD is the Cardinals Promise coin. It carries the name of a promise made in this family, and it is built the same way a promise should be: said out loud, in public, where anyone can hold you to it.

It has not launched yet. There is no CARD token on the blockchain today. Nothing is for sale. When that changes, you will read it here first.

Please don't contribute money you can't live without. I mean that as plainly as I can say it. This is not an investment, and I will never pitch it as one. If you take part, use money you'd be at peace never seeing again — the way you'd treat something you gave away, not something you're counting on.

And watch out for fakes. Until launch day, any token calling itself CARD or Cardinals Promise is not ours. Scammers copy names. The one and only official contract address will ever live in one place: this page. If it isn't posted here, it isn't ours.

What the code does

These aren't things we're asking you to believe. They're built into the contract, and on launch day you'll be able to check every one of them yourself.

  • A fixed supply. 250,000,000 CARD. No more can ever be created. The code does not contain a way to do it.
  • No special powers. No pause switch. No blacklist. No trap that stops you selling. And on launch day, ownership of the contract is given up permanently — after that, no one can change the rules. Not even us.
  • 80% goes to the public. 200,000,000 CARD enters the public trading pool at launch. We keep no hidden stash.
  • The pool is locked. The liquidity behind the coin gets locked for 12 months with a public lock anyone can inspect. We could not pull it out if we wanted to.
  • 20% is visible. 50,000,000 CARD sits in a treasury wallet named on this page. Anyone can watch that wallet, at any time, without asking us anything.

About the fee

Every trade costs 0.3%. That's a fee, and we're not going to call it anything softer. Two things worth knowing about it: it's charged by Uniswap on every coin traded there, not something we added to this one — and it stays in the locked pool rather than going to a wallet we can reach into.

We didn't want a fee that only sellers pay, or one that gets steeper if you leave early. Those exist. They're how people get trapped, and we'd rather you be able to walk away from this at any hour of any day without a penalty for changing your mind.

What we hope to do

This part is different, and we want to be honest about the difference. Everything above is enforced by code or charged by an exchange. What follows are intentions — what we mean to do, held loosely, because life is long and circumstances change.

  • Most of what this generates, we intend to give away. We're not going to put a percentage on that, or a date, because we'd rather promise less and do more than the other way around.
  • The giving we have in mind is in Lou's name, toward hospice and end-of-life care — the thing that shaped this whole story.
  • And we hope to give it widely. Not one large check to one institution — many gifts, to many families and small organizations, in many places. A family that can't cover the funeral. A hospice house that needs a new bed. The kind of giving that reaches hundreds of people rather than making one headline.
  • Some of it has to cover the real costs of doing this properly — the liquidity lock, network fees, legal and accounting. We'd rather say that out loud than pretend otherwise.
  • We'll announce gifts after they're made, not before. Receipts, not pledges.
