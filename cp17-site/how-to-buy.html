How to buy HOP

We don't recommend buying HOP, and that isn't fine print — it's our honest advice. But if you understand the risk and want to anyway, here are the steps, written for someone who has never bought a cryptocurrency and doesn't want to pretend otherwise. No jargon. About 20 minutes the first time.

Understand the risk before you start. HOP is a souvenir, not an investment, and I will never pitch it as one. The price can go to nothing. The founder's own holding is unlocked, and the pool behind the coin is small — both are spelled out on the coin page, and you should read that page before this one. If you take part, use money you'd be at peace never seeing again — the way you'd treat something you gave away, not something you're counting on. Please don't contribute money you can't live without.

HOP has not launched yet. When it does, the official address will appear on the coin page — and nowhere else.

What you'll need

Three things: an app called a wallet, a small amount of a currency called Ethereum (ETH), and the official HOP address. That's it. The steps below get you all three.

Step 1 — Get a wallet app (5 minutes)

Download Rainbow or MetaMask from your phone's app store. Both are free, and both do the same job: hold your coins and let you trade them. Those links go to the official sites — fake wallet apps exist, so use these links or your phone's app store and nothing else.

When you set it up, it shows you a list of 12 random words. That list is the only key to your money. Write it on paper. Keep it somewhere safe. Never photograph it, never email it, never type it into a website, and never give it to a human being for any reason — including someone claiming to be support, or us.

Step 2 — Buy Ethereum on Robinhood, then send it to your wallet (10 minutes)

ETH is the currency you trade for HOP, the way you'd change dollars into euros before buying something abroad. We suggest buying it on Robinhood — as of this writing it's the cheap way in, typically under 1% in spread, where an exchange's "simple buy" flow like Coinbase's can cost 2–4%. (HOP itself never trades on Robinhood or Coinbase — only the ETH comes from there.)

  1. In the Robinhood app, find Ethereum (ETH) and buy the amount you decided on, plus a few extra dollars — the extra covers the network's transaction fee, which everyone pays and which changes by the hour.
  2. In your wallet app from Step 1, tap Receive and copy your wallet's address.
  3. Back in Robinhood, open your ETH and choose Send, then paste that address. Copy and paste it — never type it by hand. The first time, Robinhood will walk you through turning on crypto transfers, which can take a day or two.

If the amount makes you nervous, send a few dollars first, watch it arrive in your wallet, then send the rest the same way.

Buying or verifying may ask for a photo ID. That's normal and required by law. And if Robinhood is more than you want to set up, your wallet app also has a Buy button that takes a debit card — it works fine, it just usually costs a little more.

Step 3 — Trade the ETH for HOP (5 minutes)

Tap Swap inside your wallet app, or open app.uniswap.org in the app's browser. Uniswap is not a company you sign up with — it's an open exchange anyone can use. That link is the official one; check the address bar says app.uniswap.org before you trade.

  1. Set the top box to ETH and type how much you want to spend.
  2. In the bottom box, paste the official HOP address from the coin page. Copy and paste it — never type it by hand.
  3. Check that the name shown is Hope Coin (HOP), then confirm.

Within about a minute, the HOP appears in your wallet. Every transfer costs 2% — written into the coin's code, going to the published treasury, unchangeable by anyone — plus Uniswap's own 0.3% on trades. There is no penalty for selling whenever you want.

If this feels too hard, that's not you failing. Stop. Nobody needs to own one of these. These apps were built by people who have never watched someone patiently try to do the right thing and get nowhere — the difficulty is theirs, not yours. Put the phone down. You've lost nothing.

Staying safe

  • One address, ever. Scammers create fake coins using real names. If an address didn't come from the coin page, it isn't ours.
  • Nobody will ever message you first. Not us, not "support," not an admin. Anyone who direct-messages you about HOP is trying to take your money.
  • Your 12 words are never needed. Not to buy, not to fix a problem, not to claim anything. A request for them is always theft.
  • Nothing is free. Giveaways, airdrops, "send 1 get 2 back" — all fake, without exception.
