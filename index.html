The Cardinals Promise coin

A promise you can check.

CARD is the Cardinals Promise coin. It carries the name of a promise made in this family, and it is built the way a promise should be: said out loud, in public, where anyone can hold you to it.

Think of it as a souvenir, not an investment. A keepsake with a promise attached, recorded somewhere anyone can check that the promise was kept. Nobody should buy one expecting to make money, and we will never suggest otherwise. Please don't spend money you can't live without — use money you'd be at peace never seeing again.

The CARD treasury wallet is now public.

We are in launch preparation, not public trading. Do not buy, swap, send funds to, or interact with a token merely because it uses the CARD name. The official CARD token contract is still listed as pending on this site.

Watch out for fakes. Scammers copy names and tickers. Until the token contract is published on cp17.org and backed by the proof board below, no address should be treated as the official CARD token contract.

Why this coin exists

Lou Brizzi was born in 1941. Into his eighties he stayed curious about technology, and — of all things — about cryptocurrency. He kept reading about it, kept asking about it, kept looking forward at an age when most people have stopped looking. He died in 2025.

CARD starts there. It is not a business idea that went looking for a story to attach itself to afterward. It is his curiosity, carried forward by his family into something that could actually be built.

This is an extension of my dad's story. I don't have to make anything up.

Rob Brizzi

That is also why this page reads the way it does. A coin made out of somebody's memory doesn't get to be vague about what it is. The costs below, the risks, the plain statement that this is a souvenir and not an investment — all of it is here because his name is on it, and we would rather tell you the unflattering version than have him attached to the other kind.

If you want to help, don't buy a coin

We want to be blunt about this, because most projects like this are not. Buying a souvenir is not a donation. If what you want is to actually help someone, give money directly to the people already doing that work. It arrives whole, it arrives faster, and none of it passes through us.

These are the places that carried this family. None has any connection to this coin, and none has endorsed it — they simply did the work, and we'd rather point you at them than at ourselves.

  • Perdido Bay Baptist Church Lou's church, on Sorrento Road. Of everything in his life, this is the one he cared about.

What the code is intended to do

The items below describe the planned CARD mechanics. Each claim must be checked against the final deployed contract and its verified source before anyone relies on it. We will replace intention with proof links as each launch step is completed.

  • A fixed supply. The planned supply is 1,000,000,000 CARD.
  • No special powers. The intended design has no pause switch, blacklist, or sell trap. Ownership status must be confirmed from the deployed contract before this is treated as final.
  • A 2% fee. The intended design applies the same 2% CARD fee in both directions and routes it to the treasury. The deployed contract must be inspected to confirm the exact implementation and whether those settings are immutable.
  • Public liquidity. Liquidity allocations and any lock must be proven with the pool and lock transactions before they are treated as complete.
  • Founder allocation. Any founder allocation will be disclosed on the ledger with the actual wallet address and on-chain balance.
  • Treasury. The current published treasury / launch wallet is 0xDAE63eBEe60A691e1538D480AE3F6509068ab300. Any final token allocation and fee flow will be confirmed from the deployed contract and on-chain balances.

What it may cost to buy one

Do not rely on this section until public trading is actually live and the pool is linked here. Network gas, exchange fees, any contract fee, slippage, liquidity depth, and price impact can all materially affect what a buyer receives and what a seller can recover.

The bigger risk is not the fee — it is liquidity and concentration. A new token can move sharply on small buys or sells. Until the live pool and holder distribution are public, there is no responsible way to characterize CARD as liquid or readily tradable.

No proof, no claim. We will not call liquidity locked, ownership renounced, source verified, or trading live until the corresponding on-chain evidence is linked from this site.
