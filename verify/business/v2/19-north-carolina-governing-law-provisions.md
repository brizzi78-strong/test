# North Carolina Governing Law Provisions

**Blue Ridge Press LLC — Professional Verification & Reference Services**

*This document is a starting template for the Company's internal use. It is NOT legal advice. Every clause below — governing law, venue, jurisdiction, and the North Carolina-specific notes — must be reviewed and confirmed by a licensed North Carolina attorney before the Company relies on or incorporates them into any agreement.*

---

## Purpose

This is a reusable clause set. It is written to be **referenced or incorporated by** the Company's other agreements (for example, the Confidentiality & Data-Handling Agreement, client service agreements, and consent forms) so that governing-law language stays consistent across documents. Where another document says "as set out in the North Carolina Governing Law Provisions," it means the clauses below.

Blue Ridge Press LLC is organized in and operates from **Raleigh, North Carolina, USA**.

---

## Clause 1 — Governing Law

> This Agreement, and any dispute or claim arising out of or relating to it or its subject matter (including non-contractual disputes or claims), is governed by and construed in accordance with the laws of the **State of North Carolina**, without giving effect to any choice-of-law or conflict-of-law rule that would apply the law of another jurisdiction.

---

## Clause 2 — Venue and Jurisdiction

> The parties agree that the exclusive venue for any action arising out of or relating to this Agreement shall be the state or federal courts located in **Wake County, North Carolina** *[confirm county with counsel]*. Each party consents to the personal jurisdiction of those courts and waives any objection based on inconvenient forum.

---

## Clause 3 — Compliance with Applicable Law

> The Company performs consent-based verification of professional references, past employment, and education only. The Company does **not** provide criminal-history, credit, or motor-vehicle-record checks, which require a credentialed consumer-reporting agency. Each party agrees to comply with all federal and North Carolina laws applicable to its role, including laws governing the collection, use, and protection of personal information.

---

## Clause 4 — Severability and Survival

> If any provision of this Agreement is held invalid or unenforceable under North Carolina law, that provision shall be modified to the minimum extent necessary to make it enforceable, or if it cannot be, severed, and the remaining provisions shall continue in full force. Provisions that by their nature should survive termination (including confidentiality, data handling, and governing law) shall survive.

## North Carolina-Specific Notes — Flagged for Counsel

The following are open questions for the Company's attorney to confirm. They are **not** conclusions and should not be treated as settled.

1. **Consumer-reporting law boundary.** Confirm that the Company's reference, employment, and education verification services, as actually performed and marketed, fall **outside** the definition of a "consumer reporting agency" and "consumer report" under the federal Fair Credit Reporting Act (FCRA) and any North Carolina analog. Confirm what consent and disclosure language the Company must use to stay outside that boundary, and how to handle client use of the Company's reports for employment decisions.
2. **Consent and disclosure.** Confirm the form, content, and retention period for subject consent (including e-signature validity) required for verifications conducted at the source.
3. **Data-breach notification.** Confirm the Company's obligations under North Carolina's identity-theft and security-breach notification law (N.C. Gen. Stat. Chapter 75, Article 2A) *[citation to be confirmed by counsel]*, including what triggers notice, timing, and content.
4. **Records retention and destruction.** Confirm how long the Company should retain consent records, source responses, and reports, and the required secure-destruction standard.
5. **Interviewing limits.** Confirm the list of questions interviewers must avoid to prevent eliciting protected-characteristic information under federal and North Carolina anti-discrimination law, so the "do not ask" notes in the interview scripts stay accurate.
6. **Contractor vs. employee classification.** Confirm proper worker classification under North Carolina and federal law for anyone performing verifications on the Company's behalf.
7. **County/venue selection.** Confirm the correct venue county and whether arbitration or mediation should be added.
8. **Insurance and accreditation references.** Confirm how the Company should describe any professional liability coverage or accreditation once obtained, and ensure no document claims coverage or accreditation the Company does not hold.
