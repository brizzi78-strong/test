# Professional Reference Interview Script

**Blue Ridge Press LLC — Professional Verification & Reference Services**

*This document is a starting template for the Company's internal use. It is NOT legal advice. The consent language and any legal or confidentiality items must be reviewed by a licensed North Carolina attorney before use.*

---

## How to use this script

This is a standardized guide for interviewing a professional reference **provided by the subject and contacted with the subject's consent**. Read the questions in order, stay neutral, and record answers verbatim where practical. Keep the call focused on professional performance. If a reference volunteers anything outside professional scope — especially anything about a protected characteristic — do not record it and steer the conversation back.

**Before the call, confirm on file:**
- [ ] Subject's signed/e-signed consent authorizing this reference contact
- [ ] Reference's name and relationship as provided by the subject
- [ ] Tracking number: **[TRACKING #]**

---

## Part 1 — Introduction and Consent Confirmation

> "Hello, my name is **[Interviewer name]** with **Blue Ridge Press LLC**, an independent professional verification service. I'm following up on a reference for **[Subject name]**, who listed you as a professional reference and has authorized us to contact you. Is now a good time for a few minutes?"

> "For accuracy, I may take notes on your answers. This is a professional reference check only — we're confirming professional performance, not collecting any personal or private information. You're free to skip any question. May I go ahead?"

- [ ] Reference confirms willingness to proceed
- [ ] Reference confirms they know the subject professionally

---

## Part 2 — Identity and Relationship

1. May I confirm your full name and current role?
2. What is (or was) your professional relationship to **[Subject name]**?
3. In what capacity did you work together (for example, manager, colleague, client)?
4. Over roughly what period did you work together? *(approximate dates are fine)*
5. How frequently did you interact in that role?

---

## Part 3 — Performance and Reliability

6. What were the subject's main responsibilities while you worked together?
7. How would you describe the quality and consistency of their work?
8. How did they handle deadlines, commitments, and follow-through?
9. Can you give an example of how they performed under pressure or handled a challenge?
10. How did they work with others — teammates, clients, or stakeholders?
11. How did they communicate and take direction or feedback?
12. Were there areas where they were still developing or that you'd flag for a future role? *(professional development only)*

---

## Part 4 — Recommendation

13. Based on your professional experience with them, would you work with or recommend this person again? Why or why not?
14. Is there anything else about their professional performance you think a prospective employer should know?

---

## Part 5 — Closing

> "Thank you — that's everything I need. To confirm, this was a professional reference for **[Subject name]**, and your responses will be summarized in a verification report. I appreciate your time."

- [ ] Thank the reference
- [ ] Record call date/time and interviewer initials
- [ ] Mark status: Confirmed / Consider / Unable to reach
- [ ] File notes against tracking number **[TRACKING #]**

---

## "Do Not Ask" Note

To keep the interview lawful and fair, **do not ask about, and do not record, any of the following** — even if the reference raises it:

- Age, date of birth, or year of graduation as a proxy for age
- Race, color, or national origin; citizenship or immigration status
- Religion or religious practices
- Sex, gender identity, sexual orientation, pregnancy, or family/marital status
- Disability, health, medical history, or workers' compensation history
- Genetic information
- Any other legally protected characteristic

Ask only about **professional performance, reliability, and conduct**. If a reference volunteers protected-characteristic information, do not write it down; note only that it was unsolicited and not used. When in doubt, move on.
