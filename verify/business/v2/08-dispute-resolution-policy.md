## 1. Purpose

This policy describes how an individual who is the subject of a verification report ("the Individual"), or a client who commissioned a report ("the Client"), may dispute the accuracy or completeness of a report the Company has produced, and how the Company handles that dispute.

The Company provides consent-based verification of professional references, past employment, and education only. The Company does not provide criminal, credit, or motor-vehicle information, and does not knowingly act as a consumer-reporting agency for those categories.

## 2. Important Legal Note (Counsel Must Confirm)

Offering a formal dispute-and-reinvestigation process — like the one described here — closely resembles obligations that the federal Fair Credit Reporting Act (FCRA) places on a **Consumer Reporting Agency (CRA)**. Adopting this policy may cause, or may be evidence that, the Company is operating as a CRA, which carries real and enforceable legal obligations (including mandatory reinvestigation, strict timeframes, accuracy duties, and consumer-notice duties).

**Before this policy is used, counsel must confirm** (a) whether the Company is a CRA under the FCRA and applicable North Carolina law, and (b) the exact obligations and deadlines that then apply. The timeframes in this document are placeholders pending that confirmation.

## 3. Scope of a Dispute

A dispute may concern:

- Factual accuracy (e.g., dates of employment, job title, degree, dates of attendance).
- Attribution (e.g., a statement recorded from a reference the Individual says did not make it).
- Completeness (e.g., a material fact the Individual says was omitted or misrepresented).
- Identity (e.g., a report matched to the wrong person).

A disagreement with a reference's honestly held opinion, where the Company accurately recorded that opinion, is documented but is not itself a factual error to be "corrected."

## 5. Intake and Acknowledgment

1. The Company logs every dispute in the Dispute Log on the date received, with a unique reference number.
2. The Company sends a written acknowledgment within **[X] business days** of receipt, confirming the dispute is under review and stating the expected timeframe.
3. If the dispute is unclear or missing information needed to investigate, the Company may request clarification; the Company documents any such request and the date.

## 6. Reinvestigation

**Mandatory obligation.** When the Company receives a dispute about the accuracy or completeness of information in a report, the Company **must conduct a reasonable reinvestigation** and either correct the information or confirm it. The Company must **complete the reinvestigation within [X] days of receiving the dispute** — the exact number of days is a legal deadline that **counsel must confirm** (for FCRA-style disputes this is commonly a fixed statutory period; the Company must meet whatever period the law requires). This is an obligation, not a courtesy: once a dispute is received, the reinvestigation and its deadline apply.

Reinvestigation steps:

1. **Isolate the disputed item(s).** Identify exactly which fact(s) are challenged.
2. **Return to the original source.** Re-contact the employer, institution, or reference of record, or an equivalently authoritative source, to re-verify the fact.
3. **Review the working file.** Compare the recorded information against the underlying notes, authorizations, and source responses on file.
4. **Consider new information.** Review any documentation the disputing party provided.
5. **Reach a determination:** confirmed accurate, corrected, or unable to verify.
6. **Document everything** — who was contacted, when, what was said, and the basis for the outcome.

If the Company cannot verify a disputed item to a reasonable standard, the Company deletes or clearly marks the item as unverifiable rather than leaving unsupported information in the report.

## 7. Outcome and Correction

Within **[X] business days** of completing the reinvestigation, the Company:

1. Notifies the disputing party in writing of the outcome (confirmed, corrected, or item removed/marked unverifiable).
2. If information was corrected or removed, issues a corrected report.
3. If a corrected report affects a decision already communicated to a Client, notifies the Client of the correction so the Client can act on accurate information. [Counsel to confirm any required notice content and recipients.]
4. Informs the disputing party of any further options counsel confirms are required to be disclosed (for example, the right to add a statement of dispute, or rights under applicable law). [Counsel to confirm.]

## 8. Records

The Company retains, for the period stated in the Record Retention Policy:

- The original dispute and any attachments.
- The Dispute Log entry and reference number.
- All reinvestigation notes, contacts, and source responses.
- The determination and the corrected report, if any.
- Copies of all correspondence with the disputing party and Client.

## 9. No Retaliation

The Company does not penalize an Individual or Client for submitting a good-faith dispute.

The Company reviews this policy at least annually and after any change in the law that counsel identifies.
