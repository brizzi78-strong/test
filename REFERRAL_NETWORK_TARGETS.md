# Referral Network Targets — Triangle & Wake Forest

The professionals who meet families before the crisis: elder law attorneys,
geriatric care managers, wealth managers, and long-term care insurance
specialists across Raleigh, Wake Forest, Durham, Chapel Hill, Cary and Apex.
These are Priority #2 in
[`LEAD_GENERATION_STRATEGY.md`](LEAD_GENERATION_STRATEGY.md): slower to build
than hospital discharge relationships, stickier once built, and their
referrals skew private-pay.

★ = start here. **WF** = office in Wake Forest. *Confirm* = address not
verified in public listings.

## Elder law attorneys (11)

**The ask:** be the placement resource they can name when a Medicaid-planning
or POA client asks "so where does Mom go?" **What you bring:** the nurse
assessment they can't provide, and families who will need their services
after a move. **Never** offer a referral fee — NC lawyers can't share fees
with non-lawyers, and the suggestion alone ends the relationship.

## Long-term care insurance

**Short version:** don't call the carriers — a national insurer's switchboard
will never refer a family to you. Two adjacent things are worth contacting.

### 1. Join the CareScout Quality Network

CareScout is Genworth's care-navigation company. Its Quality Network gives
Genworth's roughly one million LTC policyholders "no-cost guidance from a
local network of independent advisors to help identify the best fit for
senior living" — a sentence that describes this business exactly. It launched
with home care agencies and is expanding into senior living. Getting on it as
the Triangle's independent advisor puts Cardinal Care Bridge in front of every
Genworth claimant in its counties, and a claim is filed at precisely the
moment a placement decision is being made.

The nurse assessment is the asset here: LTC benefits trigger when someone
needs help with two or more activities of daily living, and families filing a
claim need exactly that documentation.

## Not a referral target

**Aging Family Solutions** (Raleigh and Durham) markets "senior care and
long-term care placement." That is a competitor, not a care manager, despite
the near-identical name to Aging Family Services. Don't confuse the two.

## Before you dial

Addresses come from firm websites, bar and NAPFA directories, and business
listings. Four are marked *confirm* because public listings were missing or
contradictory. Firms move and rebrand — Brady Cobin became the Trusts and
Estates Law Group in 2025 — so check the website the morning of a visit.

This is a starting list, not the field. The NC State Bar legal specialist
directory, the Aging Life Care Association member search, and NAPFA's
find-an-advisor tool will each surface more names for any town worth going
deeper on.
