The argument

An approval queue is a tax you pay on the reports that were never going to be a problem.

The work worth a person's attention is the missing receipt, the double-charged hotel, the dinner that ran past the cap. Everything else is a rubber stamp with a two-day wait attached. Cardinal draws that line automatically — and tells the filer which side they are on before they ever hit submit.

The usual way

File the report. Wait. A manager opens every line, finds the one problem, and sends it back. Fix it, resubmit, wait again. Finance reconciles the card statement by hand in a spreadsheet.

With Cardinal

Flags appear as you type, with the exact rule and amount. Compliant reports under the ceiling approve themselves on submit. Card charges match their expense automatically. Nobody reconciles anything.

How it works

Three steps, and two of them are automatic.

STEP 1

The charge arrives

Corporate card charges land in a feed. Enter the expense yourself and the matching charge links itself — same amount, within three days. Or file it straight from the feed in one click, with date, merchant and amount already filled in.

STEP 2

Policy checks itself

Receipt thresholds, per-category caps, stale and future dates, and duplicates across every report you have filed — all evaluated as you type, each flag written in plain language with the rule and the amount.

STEP 3

It settles

Clean reports under the ceiling approve on submit, stamped by the policy engine in the audit trail. Anything flagged goes to one approver with the reasons attached, then on to a ready-to-pay queue.

binary to run: Node, and your own machine
  • Receipts can't carry an attack. Uploads are validated end to end and rendered through the DOM, so nothing a filer submits can execute in an approver's browser.
  • Exports are safe to open. CSV cells that a spreadsheet would execute as a formula are neutralised before they reach finance.
  • Nobody approves their own spend. Owner and approver are separate by construction, and every decision is written to an audit trail that cannot be edited away.
  • Changing a budget never rewrites the past. Raising a limit applies from the month you raise it — it cannot retroactively invent money you never had.
