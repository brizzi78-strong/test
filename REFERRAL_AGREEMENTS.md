# Getting the referral agreements — the playbook

How the supply side of [`PLACEMENT_GROWTH_PLAN.md`](PLACEMENT_GROWTH_PLAN.md)
actually gets papered. Companion to [`PLACEMENT_TARGET_LIST.md`](PLACEMENT_TARGET_LIST.md)
(who to call) and the negotiation checklist in the growth plan (what to push for).

**The core fact: the communities already have the contract.** Every building
that works with A Place for Mom or CarePatrol keeps a standard "Referral
Agency Agreement" on file. You don't draft one — you ask for theirs, redline a
few terms, sign.

## 1. The packet — ready before the first call

| Item | Why |
| --- | --- |
| W-9, LLC EIN, mailing address | First thing accounts payable asks for |
| Certificate of insurance: general liability + professional (E&O), typically $1M / $2M | Some chains won't onboard without it; a few hundred dollars a year |
| One-page "who we are" (the pitch, trimmed) | Sales director forwards it to their regional |
| The referral-registration email address | The single address every family intro comes from — see step 5 |

## 2. Who to call

- **Independent building:** Sales / Marketing Director (sometimes "Community
  Relations Director"). Not the Executive Director.
- **Chain:** the local sales director either hands you the corporate agreement
  or routes you to the regional sales director — ask which. Brookdale, Sunrise,
  Carillon each have one form; sign once, covers every building.

## 3. The thirty-second script

> "I'm Rob with Cardinal Promise Care Bridge, a placement agency here in Wake
> County. We're setting up referral agreements with the communities we'll
> place families in. Could you send me your standard referral agency
> agreement — and can I come tour so I actually know the building before I
> bring anyone?"

Then the three questions: **current census · which care levels you accept ·
which agencies you already work with, and on what terms.** The last one tells
you the fee band before you've seen the paper.

## 4. What to redline (sign the rest as-is)

| Term | Their form usually says | Push for |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Fee | 75–100% of *base* rent | 100%, on the **total** monthly rate including care-level fees |
| Clawback | 90 days, any move-out | 30 days, voluntary move-out only — never death or hospitalization |
| Payment | Net 60 | Net 30 |
| Attribution | 90–180 days | 6–12 months from registration |
| Exclusivity | Sometimes requested | Never grant it |

A new agency has little leverage. Take their standard if they won't move and
**renegotiate at month 4 with move-ins behind you.** Signed at 75% beats
unsigned at 100%.

## 5. Register every family, before they tour — the step that makes it pay

Email the sales director **before** the family calls or visits: family name,
contact, care need, date. If the family reaches the community first, the
community logs them as an internet or walk-in lead and owes nothing —
agreement or not. **More agencies lose fees here than anywhere else.** Make
it a reflex: no family hears a community's name until the registration email
has gone out.

## 6. Tour the building

Not optional. It's the difference from the call center, it's the Lou test, and
sales directors sign faster for someone who showed up.

## Timelines and tracking

- Independents: same-day to one week. Chains: two to four weeks while
  corporate legal countersigns.
- One folder per community: signed PDF, effective date, fee %, clawback
  terms, attribution window, registration email, sales director's name and
  cell. Confirm the signing entity is the operator of the building, not just
  the property owner.
- Mark **Signed** on the call sheet only when you hold the countersigned copy.

## The objection you'll hear

*"We don't work with placement agencies."* →
*"You pay nothing until a family moves in, and my referrals arrive toured and
qualified. You're already paying A Place for Mom this same fee for cold
leads."* That reframe signs most of them.

## Why this fits the evening schedule

Sales directors work Saturdays because families tour on Saturdays, they
answer email at night, and the ask is a document, not a relationship. Of all
the parts of this business, this is the one that genuinely runs from the
evenings — which is why it comes first.
