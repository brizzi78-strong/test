# CARD governance — spoken summary

The token is Cardinals Promise, ticker CARD. There is one token, one contract, and one
official token site: cp17.org. Launch day is Saturday, October 4, 2026.

There are one billion CARD total, minted once at deployment. Four hundred million goes
into the public pool. Four hundred million — forty percent — is the founder's allocation,
and it stays in the founder's own wallet, unlocked. There is no vesting contract and no
timelock; that was considered and decided against. What holds the founder to a sell policy
is disclosure and a written rule, not code, and we say that in those words.

Two hundred million — twenty percent — is the treasury. It is a single wallet held by the
founder, one key, not a multisig, and its address is published on cp17.org. Every spend
from it is announced.

CARD charges a fee. On every transfer between two ordinary wallets — which means every buy
and every sell — two percent of the coins go to the treasury and the recipient gets
ninety-eight percent. Transfers to or from the treasury itself are exempt, which is how the
pool was seeded with the full four hundred million. The rate and the destination are written
into the contract: there is no setter, no exemption list, and ownership is renounced, so
neither can ever change. The fee moves coins; it never creates or destroys them. It is the
project's only built-in revenue. At two percent it covers small fixed costs like the LLC and
the website if there is trading, and it does not come close to funding professionals.

What that costs a buyer, plainly: two percent on the buy, two percent on the sell, plus
Uniswap's 0.3 percent each way — about four and a half percent for a simple round trip,
before gas, slippage, and price impact. Buyers need to set slippage to at least three
percent or the trade fails.

There is no mint function, no burn, no blacklist, and no pause switch, and ownership of the
token contract is renounced after setup, so no one — including the founder — can change any
of this afterwards.

The founder holds forty percent of supply unlocked and controls the wallet that collects
the fee. Those are the two most important facts about this token. They are disclosed
everywhere the token is described, so that anyone can check them rather than take anyone's
word for it. Any charitable giving is personal, discretionary, and never promised; the fee
is not a charity mechanism.

No board, grant, charity percentage, partner network, or legal entity is described as real
until it actually exists and has the required written, legal, and tax work behind it. The
book remains separate: buying the book does not provide CARD, and buying CARD is not a
donation.

The launch order is: counsel and tax review, independent contract review, Sepolia
rehearsal with the pool seeded from the treasury and a fee-aware buy and sell, verified
source, the six-hundred-million treasury transfer, exact pool balance, LP lock, ownership
renouncement, and only then a public contract address at cp17.org.
