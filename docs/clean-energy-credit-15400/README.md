# The Receipt as Regulation

**Form 15400, point-of-sale transfer, and the administrative life and death of the U.S. clean vehicle credits, 2008–2026**

A thesis and dissertation on IRS Form 15400, the Clean Vehicle Seller Report, and the credits it existed to document: Internal Revenue Code §30D (new clean vehicles), §25E (previously-owned clean vehicles), and §45W (qualified commercial clean vehicles).

---

## Thesis

The decisive change in U.S. clean vehicle tax policy between 2022 and 2025 was administrative rather than fiscal. The dollar amount of the new clean vehicle credit did not move: it was $7,500 in 2010 and $7,500 on the day it died. What changed was the delivery system. The Inflation Reduction Act converted a self-reported, back-loaded credit claimed months after purchase into a third-party-verified transaction reported in near real time and paid at the dealership counter, and Form 15400 is the document that carries that conversion. Filed to IRS Energy Credits Online within three calendar days of delivery and handed to the buyer as a receipt, the seller report is simultaneously an eligibility certificate, an anti-duplication register keyed to the vehicle identification number, and the settlement instruction for an advance payment to the dealer.

That redesign did three things at once, and the argument of this dissertation is that the three are inseparable. It raised effective take-up and reached buyers whom a non-refundable, deferred credit could never reach, because a discount at signing is worth more than a promise against next April's liability. It relocated the program's principal failure mode from taxpayer misreporting to dealer administrative capacity, so that a clerical omission by a seller could extinguish a statutory benefit belonging to a buyer who did nothing wrong. And it made the subsidy conspicuous: a visible price cut, itemized on a piece of paper, attributable to a named statute, which is a far easier object to repeal than a line buried in a tax return. The One Big Beautiful Bill Act terminated all three credits for vehicles acquired after September 30, 2025. The market response, a record 11.3% battery-electric share in September 2025 followed by 5.9% in October, is the cleanest natural experiment in the modern history of U.S. energy tax expenditures, and it exists in that legible form only because the credit had been made salient first.

Form 15400 is the smallest observable unit of this transformation, and reading the policy through the form rather than through the statute makes visible what the statute conceals.

## How to read this

Chapters 2 and 3 are descriptive and can be used on their own as a reference on how the seller report and the credits operated. Chapters 4 through 7 carry the argument. Chapter 8 generalizes beyond vehicles to the other point-of-sale and transferable credit regimes. Readers who want only the empirical material should read Chapter 7 and Appendix B.

## A note on authority

This is an academic and analytical document. It is not tax advice, and it is not a substitute for the Code, the regulations, or the published guidance. Where the argument rests on secondary reporting rather than primary text, the front matter says so explicitly and Appendix E lists the affected claims. Anyone acting on a live filing question should verify against the current statute, regulations, and IRS guidance.
