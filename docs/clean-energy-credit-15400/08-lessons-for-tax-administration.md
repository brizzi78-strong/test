# Chapter 8. What the form teaches about tax administration

## 8.1 The general pattern

Strip away the vehicles and the following structure remains. The government wants to deliver a benefit at the moment of a private transaction rather than after the close of a tax year. It has no presence at that transaction. So it deputizes the commercial party that is already there: registers it, requires it to certify the transaction in near real time, requires it to advance the money, and reimburses it.

This pattern is now widespread in U.S. fiscal policy. Advance premium tax credits under the Affordable Care Act pay insurers directly on the basis of projected income, reconciled at filing. Elective payment and transferability under the Inflation Reduction Act's business credits turn tax attributes into instruments that can be sold or cashed out, with registration through the same Energy Credits Online system built for vehicles. Point-of-sale delivery has been proposed repeatedly for residential energy credits under §25C and §25D, where the contractor would occupy the dealer's role, and the rebate programs funded alongside those credits already work that way.

The clean vehicle experience is the most complete natural test of the pattern in a consumer setting, from launch to termination in twenty-one months, with the failure modes documented. It is worth extracting what it teaches before the next program is built on the same template.

## 8.2 Six design principles

**1. The beneficiary must never bear the intermediary's compliance risk.** This is the first lesson and the one the vehicle credits got wrong. Where a third party's filing is a condition of a benefit, the statute must provide the beneficiary an independent route to establish entitlement: a substitute filing supported by ordinary transaction evidence, with the burden on the agency to rebut. Enforce the intermediary's obligation with penalties on the intermediary, which is what the Code does everywhere else in information reporting. Do not enforce it by extinguishing the beneficiary's substantive right.

**2. Give the beneficiary independent confirmation, immediately.** The buyer should receive an acknowledgement from the government, not only a printout from the counterparty, within days of the transaction. Detection at the moment when the counterparty is still reachable and the record fresh converts a catastrophic loss into a phone call. This is cheap, requires no legislation, and would have prevented most of the harm described in Chapter 5.

**3. Draft documentation requirements as procedural, not definitional.** Section 30D(d)(1)(H) made the seller report an attribute of the qualifying vehicle, so a missing report meant no qualifying vehicle and no possible claim by anyone. Had it been drafted as a substantiation requirement subject to reasonable-cause relief, the entire 2025 filing season problem would have been an inconvenience rather than a forfeiture. This is a drafting choice with enormous consequences and no apparent deliberation behind it.

**4. Set deadlines to the operating reality of the filer.** Three calendar days in an industry that sells on weekends, imposed on small businesses in year one of a new portal, is not a deadline calibrated to the population it governs. Business days, a short grace period, or a supervisory cure window would have cost the register almost nothing.

**5. Align the intermediary's incentive with filing in every case, not just when it is being paid.** Dealers had a strong incentive to file for transferred credits, since that is how they were reimbursed, and none at all for non-transferred ones. Whether by a small per-report administrative fee, by conditioning continued registration on a filing-completeness metric, or by requiring the report as a condition of the sale itself, the design must not leave a category of transactions where the filer's interest is indifferent and the customer's interest is everything.

**6. Close the parallel channel before opening the main one.** Section 45W let a third of the market escape the conditions attached to §30D. Any consumer credit with substantive conditions must be checked against every adjacent business provision covering the same good, because the market will route around the conditions if a route exists, and structuring a consumer transaction as a lease is not a difficult maneuver.

## 8.3 What worked and should be carried forward

The critical chapters should not obscure the fact that the core innovation succeeded.

**Real-time third-party certification is genuinely valuable.** The VIN-keyed register gave the IRS something it had never had for a consumer credit: contemporaneous knowledge of which specific asset supported which specific claim by which specific person. Duplicate and phantom claims, a live exposure under the old written-report regime, became structurally difficult. The compliance literature's central finding, that third-party reported items are reported accurately and self-reported items are not, applies to credits as much as to income.

**Effective refundability through the transfer election is the most important distributional feature of any recent consumer tax credit.** A household with no tax liability received the full amount and kept it, because the guidance declined to recapture advance payments for insufficient liability. That was achieved without amending the credit's non-refundable character, and it did more for the program's distribution than the income caps did. Any future consumer credit aimed at households below the median should replicate this, and should say so in the statute rather than leaving it to a regulation.

**Immediacy changes who participates.** The theoretical case in Chapter 4 and the evidence on liquidity constraints, salience, and take-up all point the same way, and the practical evidence from the used-vehicle segment is consistent with it. A benefit delivered at the transaction reaches people a benefit delivered at filing does not.

**A registration system creates a governable population.** Before ECO, the IRS had no roster of participating sellers, no means of suspending a bad actor, and no channel for communicating with the trade. Afterwards it had all three. The suspension and revocation authority over registration is a far more usable enforcement tool against a dealership than an examination of its customers.

## 8.4 Application to the residential energy credits

Sections 25C and 25D were themselves terminated for property placed in service after December 31, 2025, so the immediate question is moot. The design question is not, because these credits return to the legislative agenda in every energy debate and the point-of-sale proposal returns with them.

The mapping is close and the differences matter. The contractor replaces the dealer. The installed system replaces the vehicle. But there is no VIN: no manufacturer-assigned unique identifier for a completed installation, so the register key would have to be constructed from address, property, and system identifiers, which is materially harder and more error-prone. Contractors are smaller, more numerous, and more transient than franchised auto dealers, which makes both registration and the cash-flow burden of advancing the credit worse. And the installation happens over days or weeks rather than at a single moment, so "the time of sale" is not a well-defined instant.

The conclusion is not that point-of-sale delivery cannot work for home energy improvements. It is that the vehicle template cannot be copied unmodified, and that the two design failures identified here, beneficiary-borne compliance risk and definitional drafting of the documentation requirement, would be more damaging in the residential setting, not less, because homeowners have even less leverage over a contractor after the work is done than a car buyer has over a dealership.

## 8.5 Application to transferability and elective pay

The Inflation Reduction Act's business-side innovations, elective payment under §6417 and transferability under §6418, use the same registration infrastructure and raise a structurally similar question about who bears the risk when a counterparty's registration or documentation is defective. The business context differs in one important respect: the parties are sophisticated, represented, and able to allocate that risk by contract, with indemnities and price adjustments. Tax credit transfer agreements do exactly that.

That is precisely what retail buyers cannot do. A consumer buying a car does not negotiate an indemnity against the dealer's failure to file a federal form within three days. Where the parties can allocate risk by contract, the law can leave it to them. Where they cannot, the statute has to allocate it, and it should allocate it to the party that can prevent, absorb, and insure the loss. The clean vehicle credits are the case study in what happens when a design borrowed from sophisticated-party transactions is applied to retail consumers without that adjustment.

## 8.6 The record that survives

One final administrative point, addressed to whoever holds the data.

Form 15400 generated a complete, transaction-level, VIN-keyed record of a national subsidy program from its first day to its last: every qualifying sale, the seller, the buyer, the price, the date, the credit amount, and whether the credit was transferred. Matched to returns, it identifies who claimed, at what income, with what liability, and whether the advance exceeded it. Matched to registration data, it identifies which sellers participated in advance payments and which did not.

There is no comparable dataset for any other U.S. energy tax expenditure. It answers the questions this dissertation has had to leave open: the failure rate by transaction type, the distributional profile after 2023, the incremental effect of point-of-sale delivery, and the elasticity implied by the termination. Released in a disclosure-safe form, through the IRS Statistics of Income program or a secure research facility, it would support a decade of work on subsidy design.

The credits are gone. The evidence about how they worked is the only thing left worth preserving, and it is sitting in a portal that was built to process reimbursements.
