# Chapter 9. Conclusion, recommendations, and a research agenda

## 9.1 The argument restated

The clean vehicle credits were the same size in 2025 as in 2010. What changed between those dates, and what explains nearly everything of interest in the program's final two years, is that Congress moved the point of payment from the tax return to the dealership counter and built an administrative apparatus to make that possible. Form 15400 is that apparatus in its smallest visible form.

The move accomplished what it was meant to accomplish. A non-refundable credit became, for anyone who elected transfer, effectively refundable, because an advance payment was not recaptured merely because the buyer's liability could not absorb it. A deferred and uncertain benefit became an immediate and certain one, which matters most to the liquidity-constrained buyers that a deferred credit had never reached. A benefit that required predicting one's own year-end tax position became one that required signing a form. Take-up rose, and the composition of recipients almost certainly improved, though the data to demonstrate that has not been released.

The move also did three things its designers did not intend. It made a third party's clerical compliance a condition of a statutory entitlement, and, because the requirement was drafted as an attribute of the qualifying vehicle rather than as a substantiation rule, a dealer's failure to file within three calendar days destroyed a blameless buyer's credit outright. It left in place a parallel channel, §45W, through which roughly a third of the market escaped the income limits, price caps, and sourcing conditions Congress had attached to §30D, which nullified in that segment both the distributional and the industrial-policy programs the 2022 reform was built around. And it made the subsidy conspicuous: a discount handed across a desk, itemized on a government form, attributable to a named statute. Salience is why it worked and it is not unrelated to why it was easy to end.

The One Big Beautiful Bill Act terminated all three credits for vehicles acquired after September 30, 2025. Battery-electric share went from a record 11.3% in September to 5.9% in October, and first-half 2026 volumes were down roughly 24% year over year with a partial sequential recovery as manufacturers absorbed part of the withdrawn subsidy into their own margins. The paperwork outlived the credit: buyers who contracted before the deadline and take delivery afterwards still need an accepted Form 15400, filed by dealers who now have nothing at stake in filing it.

## 9.2 Recommendations

**To Congress, for any future point-of-sale credit.**

1. Draft documentation requirements as substantiation rules subject to reasonable-cause relief, never as attributes of the qualifying good.
2. Provide the beneficiary a statutory substitute-filing route supported by ordinary transaction evidence, with the burden on the agency to rebut.
3. Enforce intermediary reporting through penalties on the intermediary under the ordinary information-return regime, not through forfeiture by the beneficiary.
4. State effective refundability of an advance payment in the statute rather than leaving it to regulation, since it is the design's most important distributional feature and should not depend on an administrative choice.
5. Check every consumer credit against the adjacent business credit covering the same good, and either extend the conditions to that channel or accept in advance that the market will use it.

**To Treasury and the IRS.**

6. Send the beneficiary an independent acknowledgement of an accepted report within days, through the individual online account and by mail.
7. Count reporting deadlines in business days, with a short supervised cure window for good-faith late filing.
8. Publish participation and failure statistics for any intermediary-administered program: registration counts, filing volumes, rejection and failure rates by filer size and transaction type.
9. Keep the reporting portal open and staffed through the full limitations period for the surviving 2025 and 2026 acquisitions, and communicate directly with the buyers in that cohort, who are the most exposed population the program ever created.

**To researchers and to the Statistics of Income program.**

10. Preserve the Form 15400 population and make it available, in disclosure-safe form, matched to returns and to dealer registration status. It is the most complete transaction-level record of a U.S. energy tax expenditure that has ever existed and it now has no operational purpose.

## 9.3 Research agenda

The following are ordered by value, with the data each requires.

**1. Distribution of the credit by income, 2023–2025, separately for transferred and non-transferred claims.** Requires matched return and Form 15400 data. Tests directly whether the transfer election corrected the Borenstein–Davis concentration. This is the single most valuable analysis available and it is one query against data the government holds.

**2. Reporting failure rates by transaction type.** Requires the Form 15400 population and registration data. Tests the incentive-misalignment prediction: that dealers filed reliably when reimbursement depended on it and unreliably when only the customer's credit was at stake. Direct design implications for every future program of this type.

**3. The incremental effect of point-of-sale delivery on adoption.** Requires geographic variation in dealer participation in the advance payment program, contrasting the roughly 12,200 advance-payment registrants with the roughly 3,400 report-only registrants. Identifies the effect of the delivery mechanism as distinct from the credit itself, which is the empirical core of this dissertation's thesis.

**4. Pass-through and incidence around January 1, 2024.** Requires transaction prices by model. Point-of-sale transfer raised salience; whether it also shifted incidence toward sellers is theoretically ambiguous and empirically open.

**5. The elasticity implied by termination.** Requires monthly volume and net-of-discount transaction prices through 2026, with the manufacturer discounting that replaced part of the subsidy handled explicitly, and with hedonic controls for content. The constant-quality assumption fails here: Tesla met the withdrawal by introducing a decontented Model Y within a week rather than by moving price, so part of the adjustment ran through product specification and is invisible to a price-on-subsidy regression. Bounds are available from public data; a credible point estimate is not.

**6. Contract-date bunching at September 30, 2025.** Requires Form 15400 sale dates. The mass of contracts piled against the deadline is a direct measure of how many marginal buyers the subsidy was moving, and the manipulation that invalidates a conventional discontinuity design is itself the estimand.

**7. Section 25E as a distinct program.** The used-vehicle credit was the most progressive element of the regime and is the least studied. Take-up, failure rates, and the capacity of independent dealers to administer it all warrant separate treatment.

**8. The leasing channel.** Requires lessor-level data on §45W claims and lease terms. Measures pass-through where it was discretionary rather than mandated, and quantifies how much of the sourcing and distributional conditions the channel actually nullified.

## 9.4 A closing observation

There is a habit in tax policy of treating administration as downstream of design: Congress decides what the credit is, and the agency works out the forms. The clean vehicle credits show how badly that ordering can mislead. The most consequential decisions in this program were made in a revenue procedure and a set of regulations. Whether a household with no tax liability received $7,500 or nothing was decided by a rule about recapture of advance payments. Whether a buyer who satisfied every statutory condition received the credit at all was decided by whether a salesperson completed a portal workflow within three calendar days. Neither question is answered by reading §30D.

Form 15400 is one page and it decided both. That is the case for reading tax policy through its paperwork, and it is the reason this dissertation took a receipt as its subject.
