4. Lock vs policy — one page

Lane A strength: honest; no extra contract to get wrong. Weakness: issuer can sell into a few-thousand-dollar pool whenever they want.

Lane B strength: “could they dump on day one?” has an on-chain answer. Weakness: 80% behind a thin float; lock delays sales, does not stop them after release.

Not a lock: a website policy, a second wallet you also control, token Ownable before renounce, or an LP lock (that locks the pool, not the founder pile).

Counsel sentence: “Does an 80% position that cannot be sold until the contract says so sit better or worse than a 40% position that can be sold this afternoon, and which of those two stories are we allowed to tell the public given that both are currently in our files?”

10. Form 709 — gift tax (2026)

IRS figures. CPA signs the method before the first transfer.

Item2026
Annual exclusion per recipient$19,000
Couple, gift-splitting$38,000 to one person (both file 709 if they split)
Gift to non-citizen spouse$194,000 exclusion
Lifetime gift and estate exemption$15,000,000 per person
Top rate after exemption used40%
DueApril 15 of the year after the gift (separate from 1040)
Who filesThe donor. Recipient does not file 709 and has no gift-tax income.

Gift tax applies to digital assets. Form 709 has a digital-assets question. Filing ≠ paying. Excess over $19,000 usually just reduces the $15 million pot.

Valuation

Gift amount = FMV in dollars at the date and time of the on-chain transfer. Thin CARD pool makes last-tick × quantity easy to get wrong. 709 wants a method: appraisal or a written description. Example: 10M CARD at $0.001 = $10,000 → no 709 if that is the only present-interest gift to that person that year. Same pile at $0.003 = $30,000 → file; taxable gift $11,000; tax still $0 unless exemption is exhausted.

Recipient basis (income tax)

Bona fide gift is not income on receipt. Gain basis = donor basis + any gift tax paid. Loss basis = lesser of donor basis or FMV on gift date. No proof of donor basis → recipient basis is zero. Minted CARD basis is often near zero per token, so the built-in gain travels with the gift.

Charity vs person

Person: exclusion / exemption / possible 709. Qualifying 501(c)(3) that can custody CARD: gift-tax charitable deduction if it qualifies; income-tax deduction only with substantiation. A church that cannot hold a wallet is not a CARD gift — sell (taxable) and give dollars, or do not call it completed charity.

Tripwires

  • Many small gifts stay off 709 only if each recipient’s FMV is ≤ $19,000 that calendar year and each gift is a present interest.
  • Unused exclusion dies December 31.
  • Future interest (strings, vesting assignment) does not get the $19,000 exclusion.
  • LLC donor ≠ you. Entity gifts are a different analysis.
  • North Carolina has no separate state gift tax.
  • 709 does not bless the transfer under securities law.

Record kit for the CPA

Date, UTC, hash, from, to, quantity, FMV method and USD, donor (you vs Blue Ridge Press LLC), your basis and acquisition date, consideration (none), donee identity, present interest yes/no, public post if from a published wallet.

Question for the CPA: if unlocked CARD moves from [personal / LLC] wallet to [named person / named 501(c)(3)] on date T, how do we compute FMV in a ~$4,000 pool, do I file 709, what basis does the donee take, and who is the donor?
