# For the Board — Cardinal's Promise / CP17

**Prepared:** August 2026 · **Status:** nothing here is adopted. This is what you are being
asked to consider, and what it would mean to say yes.

---

## 1. What this is

**A memorial scholarship in memory of Lou Brizzi.** That is the purpose. Everything else in
this packet is machinery in service of it, and where machinery and purpose conflict, the
purpose wins.

**What we are asking a board to govern:** the money that comes in, who receives awards, and
whether the people running this are keeping the commitments they published.

**What has actually been decided so far:** very little, deliberately. No entity is formed, no
donation has been accepted, no award has been made, no token is deployed.

---

## 2. What we now believe, and how we got there

We spent considerable effort designing a token to fund the scholarship. **We have concluded it
is the wrong instrument, and the arithmetic that convinced us is worth showing you**, because
a board that cannot see the reasoning cannot check it.

Selling the token treasury to fund a scholarship would deliver about **$97,438** — while the
holding *appears* to be worth $1,804,252, and while taking the token's price down 99.7%. It
funds one year, once, and every dollar comes from a buyer who is left holding almost nothing.

The same goodwill given as donations delivers comparable dollars, returns tax deductions to
the givers, leaves nobody holding a loss, and **repeats every year**:

| Raised per year | Awarded after 10% operating cap | Scholarships |
|---|---|---|
| $10,000 | $8,739 | 3 × $2,500 |
| **$30,000** | **$26,217** | **10 × $2,500** |
| $50,000 | $43,695 | 17 × $2,500 |

By year four, cumulative awards pass what the token could have delivered once — and nobody
lost money to get there.

## 3. What we are asking board members to do

**Meet a few times a year and be willing to say no.** The specific functions:

- Approve the annual budget and the operating-cost figure before it is published
- Approve award criteria, and the selection of recipients
- Hold a signing position on the treasury multisig if you are asked and willing
- Ask the founder uncomfortable questions in a minuted meeting

**The last one is the actual job.** A board that agrees with the founder about everything
provides no assurance to anyone and adds no credibility to the fund.

---

## 4. What board service means, honestly

Not a formality. Directors of a charitable organisation owe fiduciary duties — broadly, to act
with **care** (attend, read, ask), **loyalty** (the organisation's interest before your own),
and **obedience** (to its stated purposes and to the law).

**Practical consequences you should weigh before agreeing:**

- **You will be named publicly.** Board membership is disclosed.
- **Directors & officers insurance should be in place before you serve.** It is budgeted at
  $500–$1,500 a year. If it is not in place, that is a reason to decline until it is.
- **Conflicts must be declared and recused, not managed informally.**
- **You may be asked to decline something you would like to approve.** See §6.

## 5. Policies proposed for adoption at the first meeting

Drafted now, before there is money attached, because that is the only time a limit costs
nothing to set. Full text in `DONATION-ROUTE.md` Appendix A.

**5.1 Operating cap.** Operating costs not to exceed **10% of contributions received** in a
fiscal year. The actual figure published annually whether above or below; any breach published
with its reason rather than smoothed. For scale: at this size operations means accounting,
Form 990 preparation, state registrations, D&O insurance, and a website — roughly
**$1,500–$4,000 a year** before anyone is paid anything. Roughly $30,000 must be raised before
the cap covers compliance alone; below that the founder covers the shortfall personally.

**5.2 Conflict of interest policy.** Written, signed annually by every director, with declared
conflicts recorded and recusal from the vote.

**5.3 Related-party exclusion.** No award to any member of the founder's family, or of any
board member's family. Adopted before the first award rather than after the first awkward
application.

**5.4 Restricted fund accounting.** Donor-designated gifts tracked separately from general
funds, so a gift given for scholarships **cannot** be spent on anything else. Segregated, not
merely labelled.

**5.5 No percentage-based fundraising compensation.** Nobody is ever paid a commission on funds
raised. This is barred by the profession's own code, is regulated in most states, and at any
realistic commission would drop the program ratio below the benchmark we cite.

**5.6 Compensation of connected persons.** Nobody connected to the fund is compensated until it
can be carried within §5.1. If that point arrives, all three limbs of the IRC §4958 rebuttable
presumption must be satisfied first: disinterested approval with recusal, comparability data
from Form 990 filings of similar organisations, and contemporaneous minutes recording the
figure, the data, the votes and the date. Two of the three is worth nothing.

**5.7 Annual reporting.** One page, four numbers: raised, operating cost, operating cost as a
percentage, awarded. **Published in bad years too** — beginning to report on a schedule and
then stopping is itself a disclosure. We do not start until we are willing to publish a year
we would rather not.

## 6. Open questions the board should know are open

Presented unresolved because a board handed only settled questions is decorative.

**6.1 Board size.** The governance record states three. A parallel workstream describes five.
Not reconciled.

**6.2 Board compensation in tokens.** The governance record allocates **5,000,000 CARD per
member over two years**. A later proposal contemplates **20,000,000 over three years** — four
times the amount on a different schedule. Both are with counsel. Note what a grant is actually
worth today: 20,000,000 marks at **$612** and would realise about **$500** if sold. **These
grants are recognition, not compensation**, and anyone accepting one should understand that
plainly rather than multiplying tokens by a price on a screen.

**6.3 Whether the token launches at all.** The launch is gated on a written demand test that
has not been met. It may never launch. That is an acceptable outcome.

**6.4 Which charitable vehicle.** Fiscal sponsor, community foundation, or our own 501(c)(3).
With counsel. The likely answer is a sponsor initially — someone who already holds the
exemption and carries the compliance burden.

**6.5 Whether family may ever receive an award.** Our position is a written exclusion (§5.3).
Counsel is being asked to confirm the instrument and scope.

## 7. What we are copying from larger charities, and what we are not

The Salvation Army directs roughly 82 cents per dollar to programs. We expect to run better
than that — 87% or above — because we are small and have almost no overhead.

**We are not copying their structure.** They are a church, which carries an exemption
unavailable by choice, and their organisation exists to run thrift stores, disaster relief and
rehabilitation centres. Their shape is a response to complexity we do not have.

**We are copying their disciplines**, which are what make a charity trustworthy at any size:
published financials, restricted-fund accounting, a written conflict-of-interest policy, an
independent board, and standard program/administrative/fundraising expense classification. All
of it is in §5. None of it is expensive. All of it is checkable by a stranger.
