# Lead Generation Strategy — Triangle Placement Service

How to fill the pipeline for the placement agency described in
[`SENIOR_CARE_PLACEMENT_STRATEGY.md`](SENIOR_CARE_PLACEMENT_STRATEGY.md),
before spending on a broad platform or paid acquisition at scale.

## The channels, ranked

| Channel | Lead quality | Cost | Speed to first lead | Verdict |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Hospital discharge planners / rehab case managers | Highest — urgent, decision imminent | Free (relationship-built) | Slow to build trust, fast once built | **Priority #1** |
| Elder law attorneys, geriatric care managers, financial advisors | High — planned, often private-pay | Free (relationship-built) | Slow to build trust | **Priority #2** |
| Local/community presence (Triangle J Area Agency on Aging, senior centers, expos) | Medium-high — trust-transferred | Low (time + a table at events) | Medium | **Priority #3** |
| Organic search / Google Business Profile / local content | Medium — self-directed families | Low (time), compounds over months | Slow to build, cheap to sustain | **Priority #4, start early** |
| Paid lead aggregators (A Place for Mom, Caring.com) | Medium — shared with competitors | High — commission or per-lead fee eats margin | Fast | **Bootstrap-only, not a foundation** |
| Paid search (Google Ads) | Medium-high — high buyer intent | High and immediate | Fast | **Hold until unit economics are proven** |

## Priority #1 — Hospital discharge planners and rehab/SNF case managers

The single highest-value referral source in this industry. A hospitalized
senior who can't safely return home puts the hospital's social work team on a
24–72 hour clock to arrange placement — whoever answers fastest and can
credibly assess availability wins a disproportionate share of that flow.

- Identify the discharge planning / case management teams at the Triangle's
  main hospital systems (Duke, UNC, WakeMed, Rex) and the skilled nursing
  facilities that do short-term rehab.
- Show up in person, leave a one-page reference sheet (not a brochure —
  something they can act on at 4pm on a Friday), and follow up regularly.
  This is relationship maintenance, not a one-time pitch.
- **Respond fast.** A missed or slow response to a discharge planner is how
  this channel disappears — reliability, not marketing spend, is what wins it.

## Priority #2 — The extended professional network

Elder law attorneys, geriatric care managers, and financial advisors see
families earlier in the process — before a crisis — and their referrals skew
private-pay, which is the healthiest kind of lead for margin. Same playbook
as above: introductions, usefulness before ask, consistent follow-up. This
network compounds slower than hospital relationships but is stickier once
built, since it isn't tied to a single discharge event.

## Priority #3 — Local presence

The [Triangle J Area Agency on Aging](https://www.tjcog.org) covers the
7-county region (Chatham, Durham, Johnston, Lee, Moore, Orange, Wake) and runs
senior centers, an ombudsman program, and community events — a natural place
to be visible without paying for it. County resource fairs and senior expos
are the same idea: low-cost, trust transferred from the host.

## Priority #4 — Organic search, started on day one

Not because it produces leads immediately — it doesn't — but because it
compounds and costs almost nothing but time:

- Claim and fill out a Google Business Profile immediately; local map-pack
  visibility for "assisted living placement [Wake/Durham/Orange county]"
  matters more than a polished website early on.
- A handful of genuinely useful local pages ("how to choose memory care in
  Durham," "what Medicaid covers for assisted living in NC") beat a generic
  brochure site — this is exactly the kind of content a placement agent
  already has expert knowledge to write.

Start this now so it has months to mature by the time paid channels are worth
considering.

## Paid aggregators — useful bootstrap, not a foundation

A Place for Mom and Caring.com generate leads immediately, but the model
works against a solo/small agency two ways: the facility (or the agent)
usually pays a placement commission back to the aggregator, and the same lead
is often shopped to multiple agents, so it's a race, not a relationship. Fine
as a short-term volume source to keep busy while the referral network is
still being built — don't build the business's identity on a channel a
competitor can outbid.

## Paid search — hold until the funnel is proven

Google Ads on high-intent local terms ("memory care near me," "assisted
living placement Raleigh") converts well, but it's expensive and immediate —
exactly the kind of spend the placement-first strategy says to avoid before
the fundamentals are validated. Worth revisiting once cost-per-placement from
the free/relationship channels is known, so ad spend can be judged against a
real baseline instead of a guess.

## Track it in the internal tool, not a spreadsheet

This is the "smallest software feature" moment from the placement strategy:
the first useful build isn't a marketing platform, it's a lightweight way to
log **where each lead came from** (which discharge planner, which attorney,
which event) and what it converted to. That single field — referral source —
is what turns "we should call more hospitals" into "Duke Rehab sent us 6
families and 4 placed; WakeMed sent 2 and 0 placed, go find out why."
Without it, the whole point of ranking channels above is guesswork.

## Recommendation

Spend the first 60–90 days entirely on Priorities #1–#3 (relationships and
local presence) plus starting the SEO groundwork — all low-cost, and the two
relationship channels are also literally how the placement agency validates
whether it has a real business before writing more software. Use a paid
aggregator only if the pipeline is thin and cash flow needs it, and hold paid
search until there's a real cost-per-placement number to spend against.
