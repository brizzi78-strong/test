# SNF Medicare Advantage Management Software — Sales Messaging

NLP-based marketing framework: match the buyer's language, emotions, and
decision process — **without manipulation**. Every claim below must stay
true to what the product actually does. If a line wouldn't survive a demo,
it doesn't ship.

**Buyer:** SNF administrators, business office managers, MDS coordinators,
case managers, and regional directors of revenue cycle who live inside the
Medicare Advantage authorization grind every day.

---

## The message structure

### 1. Start with the pain (pace their reality)

> Medicare Advantage plans are creating more authorizations, denials,
> documentation requirements, and unpaid administrative work for skilled
> nursing facilities.

This is *pacing*: stating something the buyer already knows is true, in
the words they'd use themselves. No exaggeration needed — the reality is
painful enough on its own.

### 2. Reflect their experience (they feel seen)

> You should not need spreadsheets, sticky notes, and repeated phone calls
> just to determine whether a resident's stay is authorized.

This names the *daily texture* of the problem — the sticky note on the
monitor, the third call to the plan this week, the spreadsheet only one
person knows how to read. When a buyer hears their own Tuesday described
back to them, trust goes up because accuracy went up.

### 3. Create a clear future picture (lead to the outcome)

> Imagine opening one dashboard and immediately seeing every resident's
> authorization status, covered days, documentation deadlines, appeals,
> and financial exposure.

Concrete, visual, and checkable. Not "revolutionize your workflow" —
a specific picture the buyer can verify against the actual product in
the first five minutes of a demo.

### 4. Position the software as the bridge

> Our platform helps SNFs manage Medicare Advantage cases from admission
> through discharge — reducing missed deadlines, preventing avoidable
> denials, and helping teams capture the reimbursement they earned.

The product is the bridge from step 2 (current state) to step 3 (future
state). Note the framing: *the reimbursement they earned*. The facility
already delivered the care. This is about not losing what's already theirs.

### 5. Use outcome-focused language

Never sell "software." Sell what changes:

- **Fewer denials**
- **Faster authorizations**
- **Better interdisciplinary communication**
- **Reduced administrative labor**
- **Clearer revenue forecasting**
- **Fewer residents remaining without confirmed coverage**

## Core sales statement

> **Every Medicare Advantage day your team can't confirm is a day you may
> never get paid for. Our platform gives your facility one place to see
> every resident's authorization status, covered days, documentation
> deadlines, and appeals — from admission through discharge — so your team
> stops chasing plans by phone and spreadsheet, and starts capturing the
> reimbursement you already earned.**

### Short version (elevator / cold open)

> We help skilled nursing facilities stop losing Medicare Advantage
> revenue to missed deadlines and avoidable denials — one dashboard,
> every resident, admission through discharge.

### One-liner (tagline weight)

> Know every covered day. Keep every earned dollar.

## Language-matching guide (the "NLP" part, done ethically)

**Use their vocabulary, not ours.** Buyers say "auth," "covered days,"
"denial," "peer-to-peer," "NOMNC," "cut letter," "the plan." They do not
say "workflow optimization solution." Match their words exactly.

**Match the decision channel:**

- *Visual buyers* ("show me," "I need to see it"): lead with the
  dashboard. "See every resident's status at a glance."
- *Auditory buyers* ("that sounds right," "talk me through it"): lead
  with communication. "No more phone tag with the plan — everyone on the
  team hears the same status."
- *Kinesthetic buyers* ("it feels chaotic," "we're drowning"): lead with
  relief. "Get the sticky notes off the monitor and the weight off your
  case managers."

**Pace, then lead.** Steps 1–2 state verifiable truths the buyer already
lives (pacing). Only after agreement is earned do steps 3–4 introduce the
future state (leading). Never lead first.

**Emotion to engage:** the quiet frustration of doing unpaid work to get
paid for real work — and the professional pride of a clean, defensible
revenue cycle. That's the honest emotional core; use it.

### What we never do

- **No manufactured fear.** The pain in step 1 is real and documented;
  we state it, we don't inflate it.
- **No false scarcity or urgency.** No fake deadlines, no "only 3 spots."
- **No unverifiable claims.** Every outcome in step 5 must be traceable
  to a product capability or a real customer result.
- **No exploiting distress.** Facilities in financial trouble get honest
  scoping, not pressure.
