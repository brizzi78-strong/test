One document. Everything decided, in the order it gets done. The detail lives

## The business in one paragraph

**Memory care placement, Wake County.** Free to families; paid by communities
at 100% of the first month's total charges; no building signed under $6,500 a
month. Two founders — a Certified Dementia Practitioner and a registered
nurse — who tour with every family and answer within the hour. Supply signed
before demand is chased. Referrals earned from the people who cannot be paid.
Every recommendation passes one test: *would Lou call this place home?*

## Non-negotiables

1. **100% of total first-month charges in every agreement. No building under $6,500/month.** "No" is logged as "not yet."
2. **Clawback: 30 days, voluntary move-out only.** Never on death or hospitalization.
3. **Register every family with the community before they make contact.** No registration, no fee — make it a reflex.
4. **Never pay anyone clinical per referral** — physicians, hospital, SNF, home health, hospice, care managers, attorneys. Earned only.
5. **Say the disclosure line early and unprompted:** communities pay our fee, families never do, and we tell you in writing which ones pay us.
6. **Free before move-in, optional and family-paid after.** No placement fee on any re-placement of an advocate family.
7. **No resident on camera, no family's story with identifying details, no placeholder testimonials.**
8. **The brand is the face.** Linda and the partners front; the founder's name isn't amplified.
9. **Attorney before money moves** with any partner, advisor, or investor.
10. **The Lou test on every placement.** It costs a fee sometimes. It's why the fees keep coming.

## Months 4–6 — Make it repeatable

- Day-90 pilot decision: JV priced on the log, handshake continues, or part as friends.
- **Return to every building that said "not yet."** Move-ins behind you, inventory beside you.
- Grow to 40–60 agreements; add Johnston and Chatham as families pull you there.
- 2–4 placements a month. 90-day retention tracked; it's what earns the full rate and prevents clawbacks.
- Advocate line: first 5 families. Monthly visit, care conference, written update to the kids in Ohio.
- Workshop monthly, rotating attorneys. Podcast biweekly; every guest a referrer.
- Charles: the South Florida playbook — the same sequence, his market, his face. Florida attorney first (Patient Brokering Act).
- Frustration log started and kept.

## Months 7–12 — Scale, then choose

- 4–8 placements a month; the obvious memory care call in the Triangle.
- Advocate line to 10 families ($36–54K/year recurring).
- Referral compounding: placed families referring families, 15–25% of inquiries by month 9.
- SEO pages ranking; reviews compounding.
- The three doors at month 12: stay solo and optimize; add an advisor (only at a sustained 8+/month); or the software — only with twelve months of frustration-log data and twenty agent interviews.

## Reaching the wealthiest families

Affluent families don't search for placement. They ask three people: their
advisor, their doctor, and a friend at the club. The strategy is to be the
name those three say.

**Where they are.** The entry-fee CCRCs, whose residents are wealthy by
definition and whose adult children are the buyers: Springmoor, Carol Woods,
Carolina Meadows, Galloway Ridge at Fearrington, Croasdaile, Searstone,
Glenaire, The Cardinal at North Hills. Fearrington Village and Governors Club
in Chatham/Orange; Hayes Barton, Country Club Hills, and North Hills in
Raleigh; Preston and MacGregor Downs in Cary; Hope Valley in Durham.

**Who they call first, in order:**

| Gatekeeper | Why they get the call | The way in |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Wealth managers and RIAs** — CAPTRUST and First Citizens Wealth are headquartered in Raleigh; the private-wealth teams at the national firms | "Dad needs care" is a money question before it's a care question. Fiduciary rules forbid referral fees — earned only | A 30-minute "what memory care costs and how families pay for it" briefing for the advisor team; then the podcast |
| **Concierge and direct-primary-care physicians** | The first call when Mom declines; their patients are older and affluent | Linda's FL-2 speed and the one-hour response — the doctor looks good when the family lands well |
| **Private-wealth attorneys and bank trust officers** (Smith Anderson, TrustCounsel, First Citizens and Truist trust departments) | They hold the family's whole picture and the kids' phone numbers | The RN Care Advocate is the product they refer first; placement follows |
| **CPAs** | The long-term-care insurance and spend-down conversations run through them | Same briefing as the wealth managers |
| **Luxury and senior-specialist realtors** (SRES) | Selling the house is the trigger for the move | Reciprocal: you send the listings, they send the families |

**Rooms.** Country club lifelong-learning luncheons (Carolina Country Club,
North Ridge, MacGregor Downs, Prestonwood, Governors Club, Hope Valley,
Chapel Hill CC) — the route in is a member, which is what a physician partner
or an elder law attorney is for. Alzheimer's Association gala committees and
the Duke and UNC hospital foundation events: sponsor a table once a year and
you're seated with the demographic. Large churches with affluent
congregations, through the care pastor.

**Who fronts it.** Linda, with the book and the RN; the workshop upgraded to
an "estate and care planning evening" co-hosted with a wealth manager and an
elder law attorney; podcast guests drawn from the table above. The founder's
role is the introductions and the follow-through, which needs no face.

## The attorney agenda — one engagement

1. NC placement / referral-agency licensing and family fee disclosure.
2. The referral agreement template: the 100%-of-total-charges definition, the clawback clause, attribution.
3. Physician advisor structure: flat fee or equity, never per head.
4. Connected investors: passive equity, disclosure, no steering.
5. The private duty JV with a physician-owned company; his patients as a bright line.
6. RN Care Advocate: scope (advocacy, not hands-on nursing), liability coverage, the no-fee-on-re-placement rule.
7. Worker classification for any bird dog or part-time advisor.
8. Florida: Patient Brokering Act, before Charles signs anything.
