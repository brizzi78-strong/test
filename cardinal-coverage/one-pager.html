The problem

More than half of Medicare is now administered by private plans that each run their own utilization management. Facilities absorb that shift as unpaid administrative labor: 29% of nursing homes report Medicare Advantage denials every single day, another 37% weekly, and 67% have had a resident's coverage pulled too soon during the stay — often against medical advice. Some plans reauthorize in three-day increments, roughly seven submissions on a twenty-day stay. The work is tracked today in spreadsheets, sticky notes, and repeated phone calls.

The insight — this is a workflow problem, not a clinical one

The denials are largely wrong. HHS OIG found in 2026 that MA organizations overturned nearly all appealed prior-authorization denials for SNF admission. Independent QIO physician reviewers rejected 48% of MA skilled-nursing denials, and 77% of repeat termination notices — of which there are many, since 27% of notices are repeats within the same stay, averaging 2.7 per resident. MA members are about 52% of the Medicare population in the reviewed region yet generate 93% of SNF appeals. Money is being lost for procedural reasons, which is precisely what software fixes.

The product

Deadline engine

Authorization covered-through, next submission due, NOMNC deliver-by, QIO fast-track cutoff, physician recertification, and weekly progress note — per resident, colour-coded, sorted most-urgent-first, with live financial exposure.

Appeal workflow

Assembles the regulatory argument around the facility's clinical record: burden-of-proof appeals to the QIO, plan reconsiderations on the Traditional Medicare criteria floor, and challenges to short-interval authorization.

The wedge: not another EHR. The EHR holds the chart; it does not run the payer fight. Cardinal Coverage is the layer in between — today a spreadsheet that one person maintains.

Why now

  • The legal floor exists and is unexploited. CMS-4201-F (2024) bars MA plans from applying criteria more restrictive than Traditional Medicare; 42 CFR 422.138 ties authorization duration to the treating provider's recommendation; 42 CFR 422.626 puts the burden of proof on the plan in a termination appeal.
  • CMS-0057-F is already biting. Since January 2026, plans must decide in 72 hours expedited / 7 days standard with a specific reason on every denial, and must publish prior-authorization metrics.
  • The data pipe opens January 1, 2027. Every MA plan must run a production Prior Authorization FHIR API. The manual portal-chasing that makes this hard today becomes an integration — rewarding whoever already owns the facility-side workflow.

Competition

EHR platforms (PointClickCare, MatrixCare) record authorization numbers and coverage dates as a billing field that catches gaps at claim time — no NOMNC clock, no QIO deadline, no appeal. RCM analytics (SNF Metrics, primeCLAIMS, Inovalon) are retrospective: they report what was lost. Outsourced prior-auth BPOs prove demand but scale cost linearly and leave the facility owning the deadline. Everyone else is downstream of the denial; we are upstream of it.
