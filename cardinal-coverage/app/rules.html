A stale rule is more dangerous than no rule. Every entry carries the source it came from, the date a human last confirmed it, and a review-by date. Entries past review turn amber, then red — treat those as unverified until someone re-checks the source. Nothing here is scraped or inferred; each seeded rule was read off the cited source on the date shown. Plan operational details change without notice and vary by state, product line, and contract year, so confirm anything load-bearing against the current provider manual before you act on it.

Filter

How freshness works. Each rule has a review interval — 90 days by default, shorter for things that change often like fax numbers and portal routes, longer for codified federal regulation. Past the review date a rule is due; at twice the interval it is stale. The badge reflects the last human confirmation, not the last edit, so correcting a typo does not reset the clock.

Change history is kept. When a rule's value changes, the previous value is retained with the date it was superseded. That history is the point: it is how you show a plan that its own requirement moved, and how a new hire learns what the rules were during a stay they are appealing months later.

This is a local prototype. Data lives in this browser only. A production registry needs a shared backend, per-source monitoring, and a named owner for each plan — see docs/rules-registry.md for what that would take. Not clinical, legal, or billing advice.
