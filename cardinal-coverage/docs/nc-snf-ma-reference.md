# North Carolina SNF · Medicare Advantage PT Filing & Documentation Reference

Working reference for Aetna and WellCare Medicare Advantage cases in a North
Carolina skilled nursing facility. Verified August 2026. Plan-specific
procedures change between contract years — reconfirm portal routes and
turnaround times against each plan's current provider manual.

Not clinical, legal, or billing advice.

### Aetna Medicare Advantage — North Carolina

Submit through the **Availity provider portal** (Aetna's standard route) or
your EDI vendor using the existing Precertification Request transaction.
Confirm the service against Aetna's current participating provider
precertification list before assuming a route.

Aetna provider line: **888-632-3862**

> **Do not route NC through EviCore.** Effective January 1, 2026, Aetna moved
> post-acute care (SNF and inpatient rehab) utilization management to EviCore
> via the MedSolutions portal — but **only for New Jersey, New York,
> Pennsylvania, and West Virginia** (excluding NJ FIDE). North Carolina is not
> in that program. National guidance and vendor materials describing the
> EviCore post-acute route do not apply to your cases, and sending an NC
> request there will cost you days.

### WellCare Medicare Advantage — North Carolina

Submit through the **secure Provider Portal**: log in, open the **Care
Management** tab, select **Create New Authorization**. Fax and phone are
accepted for expedited/urgent requests only.

Check requirements first in WellCare's **NC authorization lookup tool** and
the **North Carolina Prior Authorization Guide**, and work from the 2026
Medicare Advantage Provider Manual.

> **Watch the product line.** WellCare runs both a Medicaid Standard Plan and
> Medicare Advantage in North Carolina, on different sites with different
> rules. `marketplace.wellcarenc.com` is not the Medicare Advantage path.
> Confirm you're in the Medicare provider area before you file.

### Timing, both plans

| Stage | When |
| --- | --- |
| Initial authorization | At or before admission — never after |
| Concurrent clinical update | Every 5–7 days, filed **before** the covered-through date |
| Physician certification | At admission, first recert by **day 14**, then every 30 days |

A late concurrent update is functionally a denial you handed them. A missing
or unsigned physician certification is a clean technical denial and the
easiest kind to lose.

## 4. PT evaluation: what must be established

**Prior level of function.** The most under-documented item and the most
common reason a reviewer writes "not medically necessary." "Independent" is
not enough. Lived alone in a two-story home, drove, managed stairs without a
rail, ambulated in the community without a device. *The gap between that and
today is the medical necessity argument.*

**Current status in objective numbers.** Berg Balance, Timed Up and Go, gait
speed, distance ambulated with assist level, Section GG. Standardized
measures beat narrative every time.

- ❌ "Patient is improving with therapy."
- ✅ "Ambulates 45 ft with RW, min assist — from 15 ft, max assist on admission."

**Goals that are measurable, functional, and time-bound**, each tied to the
discharge environment.

- ❌ "Improve lower extremity strength."
- ✅ "Ambulate 100 ft with RW, supervision level, to negotiate distance from
  parking area to apartment door, within 14 days."

**Frequency, duration, intensity**, plus both the treating and underlying
medical diagnoses.

## 5. Daily treatment notes

The test is whether the service **required the skills of a qualified
therapist**. Notes that describe what the patient did — rather than what the
therapist did — get denied.

Document the clinical decision-making: the mid-session assessment, the type of
cueing given, the parameters modified and why, the safety judgment exercised,
the gait deviation identified and corrected.

Include every time: date, time in/out, total treatment minutes, specific
interventions with parameters, patient response.

**Explain every missed day.** Refusal, illness, medical appointment — say so.
An unexplained gap in the 5-day schedule reads as "didn't need skilled care
that day."

**Never clone notes.** Five days of "patient tolerated treatment well,
continue POC" is the fastest route to a cut letter, because it is affirmative
evidence that nothing skilled was happening.

---

## 6. Weekly progress note — the document that decides coverage

Needs objective movement toward the stated goals plus an explicit
justification for continued skilled care.

**If the resident has plateaued, do not write "plateaued" and stop.** Pivot to
the maintenance rationale under *Jimmo v. Sebelius*: skilled services remain
necessary to maintain function or prevent deterioration, and this specific
patient's condition makes an unskilled program unsafe or ineffective — here is
why. Improvement is **not** required for skilled coverage. Both plans will
still cut on it. That is the denial you win.

## 7. Two arguments that win NC appeals

**CMS-4201-F (effective January 1, 2024).** MA plans must apply Traditional
Medicare coverage criteria and may not use internal criteria more restrictive
than 42 CFR 409.31–409.35 where Medicare's rules are fully established. An
approved authorization must remain valid for the full course of medically
necessary treatment. **When a denial arrives, request the specific criteria
applied.** If it's a stricter internal guideline, that is the appeal.

**Jimmo v. Sebelius.** No improvement standard. "Plateaued," "no further
progress," and "maintenance level" are not valid grounds for termination when
skilled services are still needed to maintain function or prevent decline.

## 8. Why you should appeal — and appeal again

From Acentra's own CMS-approved study of the region that includes North
Carolina, plus the 2026 HHS OIG report:

- MA members are roughly **52%** of the Medicare population in Acentra's
  regions but generate **93%** of SNF-based appeals.
- Acentra physician reviewers **disagreed with the plan's denial 48% of the
  time** — higher than the 42% rate for Traditional Medicare appeals.
- **27%** of MA notices of non-coverage were *repeats* within the same stay,
  averaging **2.7 repeat notices per beneficiary**. One patient received
  **12 notices** after winning previous appeals.
- On those repeat appeals, reviewers sided **against the plan 77% of the
  time**.
- HHS OIG (2026): MA organizations **overturned nearly all** appealed prior
  authorization denials for SNF admission — which is itself evidence the
  initial denials were wrong.

**The operational takeaway:** a first denial is close to a coin flip, and a
re-issued NOMNC after you already won is a better-than-three-in-four bet in
your favor. Appealing is the default, not the exception. Build the
documentation above so the reviewer has something to rule on.

## 9. North Carolina resident and family support

**NC SHIIP** (Seniors' Health Insurance Information Program, NC Department of
Insurance) — free, unbiased Medicare counseling with trained counselors in
every NC county. Useful when a family needs help navigating an appeal.

- Toll free: **855-408-1212** · Local: 919-807-6900
- Email: ncdoi.ncshiip@ncdoi.gov · Web: ncshiip.com

SHIIP counselors are not insurance agents and do not sell or endorse plans.
The NC Long-Term Care Ombudsman program operates through the regional Area
Agencies on Aging if a resident needs advocacy beyond the coverage question.
