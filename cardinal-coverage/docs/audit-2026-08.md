## Verdict

The underlying thesis survives. The regulatory floor is real, the OIG and Acentra data exist and were transcribed accurately, the category insight is defensible, and the internal gap analysis is unusually honest. **What did not survive is the execution layer: nearly every citation attached to the thesis is wrong at least once, and the deadline engine — the entire product — computes two of its three statutory deadlines one full day late.**

**Single most dangerous finding:** `tracker.html` line 275 defines `effective = addD(covered, 1)`. On the CMS-10123 NOMNC the Effective Date *is* the last covered day, not the day after. Every downstream clock is therefore off by one in the direction that forfeits rights: the tracker tells a facility to deliver the NOMNC a day too late (invalid notice, facility liability) and tells a family the QIO fast-track window closes at noon on a day when it has already closed. Missing that noon cutoff is unrecoverable. Corroborated independently by four agents; two more found the same product shipping *three different answers* to this deadline across the code, the footer, the reference panel, and `docs/nc-snf-ma-reference.md`.

**Audit reliability caveat, applies to everything below:** ecfr.gov, law.cornell.edu, cms.gov, federalregister.gov, govinfo.gov, hhs.gov, oig.hhs.gov and kff.org were blocked by the egress proxy for the entire session, and the WebSearch budget was exhausted. No agent read a primary source directly. Every CFR pincite below is triangulated from search-result extracts and secondary analysis. **Nothing in the CRITICAL section should ship into a payer letter until one person with unrestricted network access re-verifies it against eCFR.**

The rule is at **42 CFR 422.112(b)(8)(i)(A)** (Access to services — continuity of care), placed there by CMS-4201-F alongside the 90-day transition provision.

### C2. Both notice deadlines are computed one day late
*Corroborated by 4 agents; a 5th independently found the same three-way internal contradiction.*

`tracker.html:275`
```js
const effective = addD(covered, 1);   // WRONG — "first non-covered day"
```
CMS-10123 instructions define the Effective Date as "the actual date coverage of the service will end" — the **last covered day**. CMS's own worked example: last covered day Friday → NOMNC delivered no later than Wednesday, QIO call by noon Thursday. The tracker currently produces Thursday and noon Friday.

Fix `tracker.html:275-279`:
```js
const effective = covered;   // CMS-10123 Effective Date = last covered day
out.push({ lab:"NOMNC deliver by", date:addD(effective,-2), days:diffD(addD(effective,-2),t) });
out.push({ lab:"QIO fast-track deadline", date:addD(effective,-1), days:diffD(addD(effective,-1),t), noon:true });
```
Fix `tracker.html:149` footer — currently "The effective date of non-coverage is the day after that." Replace with: *"'Covered through' is the last covered day, which is the Effective Date printed on the NOMNC (CMS-10123). NOMNC deliver-by is 2 calendar days before that date. The QIO fast-track deadline is noon on the day before that date."* Delete the old sentence.

Re-run the demo caseload after this change; the seeded note on case 214-B ("QIO fast-track deadline is today at noon") will need updating.

### C3. The fast-track deadline is anchored to the wrong event
*Corroborated by 3 agents. Compounds C2 — fix both together.*

42 CFR 422.626(a)(1)/(b)(1) runs the clock from **delivery of the notice**: request to the IRE "by noon of the first day after the day of delivery of the termination notice." The NOMNC form prints the effective-date version. The two coincide **only** when the notice is delivered at the two-day minimum, and 422.624(b)(1) is a floor, not a ceiling — early delivery is compliant and moves the regulatory deadline earlier. A tool that anchors solely to the effective date will show green after the 422.626 clock has run.

- Add a required field: **"Date NOMNC delivered to resident."**
- Compute both dates; drive urgency colour off the **earlier**: `min(noon of effective−1, noon of delivery+1)`.
- `tracker.html:124` and `:149`, `docs/nc-snf-ma-reference.md:28-29`, `README.md:101-103`: *"The deadline printed on the NOMNC is noon on the day before the effective date. 42 CFR 422.626(a)(1) separately requires the request by noon of the first day after the notice was delivered. These are the same date only when the notice was delivered exactly two days out; where they differ, act on the earlier."*
- Purge the phrase "coverage ends" from all deadline prose — the tracker's own glossary defines "covered through" as the last covered day, so "noon the day before coverage ends" means two different things on two lines of the same page.

### C4. Missing the deadline no longer forfeits the appeal (and neither does discharge)
*Corroborated by 3 agents. Currently absent from every file.*

CMS-4205-F, effective **January 1, 2025**, amended 42 CFR 422.624/422.626 so that (1) the **BFCC-QIO, not the plan**, must review **untimely** fast-track appeals of SNF/CORF/HHA terminations, and (2) the forfeiture-on-leaving-the-facility provision was **fully eliminated**. Since January 1, 2025 (CMS-4205-F, amending 42 CFR 422.626), the BFCC-QIO must accept and decide an untimely fast-track request, and an enrollee no longer forfeits the appeal by leaving the facility. What is lost is the guaranteed decision timeframe and the automatic financial-liability protection — not the review."*
- Change the tracker's QIO clock label from a terminal cutoff to *"QIO fast-track deadline (late filings still accepted — protections lapse)."*
- `investors.html:173` currently reads "Revised NOMNC/DENC forms (2025) expanded enrollee fast-track appeal rights." Forms do not expand rights. Replace: *"**CMS-4205-F (Jan 1, 2025).** Amended 42 CFR 422.624/422.626: the BFCC-QIO must now review untimely fast-track appeals, and enrollees no longer forfeit appeal rights by leaving the facility. ### C5. The facility has no standing to file Letter B
*`appeal-letters.html` letterRecon() line 237, sign() line 218. Two agents, independently, same conclusion.*

Parties to a reconsideration under 42 CFR 422.574 are the enrollee (or appointed representative), an **assignee** — a provider who has furnished the service and formally waived any right to payment from the enrollee — the estate's legal representative, or an entity with an appealable interest. 42 CFR 422.578 limits requests to parties plus a treating physician for pre-service requests. A DON on facility letterhead is none of these. A plan can dismiss on this ground alone without touching the merits, burning the 60-day window.

Separately, 42 CFR 422.584(a) and (c)(2): expedited reconsideration is triggered by **the enrollee or a physician**. Under (c)(2)(i) an enrollee request is expedited only "if [the plan] determines" the standard is met — discretionary. Under (c)(2)(ii), where a physician indicates it, the plan **must** expedite. The letter's boilerplate "EXPEDITED REVIEW REQUESTED" over an RN signature secures nothing.

**Fix:** add a required **Filing capacity** selector that gates letter generation:
1. *Appointed representative* → inserts "This request is filed by [name] as the enrollee's appointed representative. A completed Form CMS-1696 is enclosed." and adds CMS-1696 to enclosures.
2. *Physician request* → adds a physician signature block; only this option renders the expedited header.
3. *Non-contracted provider as assignee* → inserts "The facility is a non-contracted provider and encloses a signed Waiver of Liability Statement waiving any right to payment from the enrollee for the services at issue, under 42 CFR 422.574(b)."

Add a physician-support block to Tab B regardless, and a UI warning when it is blank: *"Without physician support, the plan has discretion to refuse expedited handling under 42 CFR 422.584(c)(2)(i)."*

Also fix the standard recited at line 237: the letter says "would seriously jeopardize the enrollee's health **and** ability to regain maximum function." The regulation (422.584(c)(2)) says "**could** seriously jeopardize the **life or** health of the enrollee **or** the enrollee's ability to regain maximum function." The letter converts a disjunctive test into a conjunctive one against itself. Use the regulatory phrasing verbatim.

### C12. "Keep PHI out of this tool" is legally wrong and blocks the sale
*Corroborated by 3 agents (buyer lens, two compliance lenses).*

`tracker.html:114`, `outcomes.html:109`. Under Safe Harbor (45 CFR 164.514(b)(2)) **all elements of dates** related to an individual are identifiers — the tracker is built on admission, covered-through, certification and denial dates, so it can never satisfy Safe Harbor regardless of the label. Initials are a name-derived code, forbidden as a re-identification code under 164.514(c); a room number locates the individual inside the building. Add plan name, dollar exposure and free-text clinical narrative and the record is trivially re-identifiable. The tool's own `README.md:164` concedes "any production version would need a real HIPAA posture" — the banner contradicts the README.

The practical harm is not the label: a facility told "this isn't PHI" skips the risk analysis, the workstation controls, and the breach assessment the record actually requires.

Replace both banners:

> "This tool holds PHI. Case identifiers plus service dates are protected health information even when you use initials or a room number — coded data is not de-identified data (45 CFR 164.514(b)-(c)). It does not meet Security Rule requirements for access control, audit logging, or encryption at rest. Use a facility-owned device with full-disk encryption and your own login, lock the screen when you step away, and confirm with your compliance officer before entering resident data."

### C16. `docs/gap-analysis.md` gets the HIPAA reasoning wrong, and contradicts itself
*Two agents.*

Lines 122-124 claim instrumenting the founder's own facility "requires no HIPAA build, because a covered entity using a tool on its own residents is a fundamentally different posture from becoming a business associate." The posture difference is real as to **who is liable** and false as to **whether controls are required**. The Security Rule applies to all ePHI a covered entity maintains (45 CFR 164.302, 164.306(a)) — risk analysis, unique user ID (164.312(a)(2)(i), required), audit controls (164.312(b), required), backup/contingency (164.308(a)(7), required), media disposal (164.310(d)(2), required). The work is re-billed to the facility's compliance officer, undocumented.

The document also contradicts itself: lines 74-75 state a localStorage page "cannot satisfy any of it, and no facility's compliance officer will sign for it," then fifty lines later recommends running exactly that on real residents.

Lines 68-70 state the BA trigger wrongly in both directions: *"The moment a second facility's resident data touches software, the vendor becomes a business associate."* BA status turns on **access to PHI**, not customer count (45 CFR 160.103). If the software entity is legally distinct from the founder's facility and maintains that facility's PHI, BA status attaches at facility **one**. Conversely a purely client-side tool the vendor never accesses may not create BA status at facility fifty. The direct-liability-under-HITECH half is correct.

## (d) STRATEGIC — the substantive challenges

### S1. The TAM does not close, and slide 8 is blank because of it
Slide 10's own anchor ("a single avoidable denial on a twenty-day stay can exceed a year of software cost") plus the tracker's $4,980 demo exposure puts ACV around $6-12k/facility/year. 419 NC facilities × $10k = $4.2M for the entire beachhead at 100% share; ~14,700 nationally × $10k = ~$150M TAM at 100% of every certified nursing facility in America. Realistic ceiling is $30-50M ARR under heroic penetration. Leaving the slide blank does not defer the question — it advertises it, and the speaker notes ("do not inflate," "investors punish TAM you cannot derive") show the founder senses it.

Three real options, pick one and show the derivation: (1) reprice as a share of recovered dollars (15-25% contingency) — raises revenue per facility 5-10x but converts this to a services business with services multiples, so say so; (2) widen the unit from SNF-only to all post-acute prior authorization (home health, IRF, LTACH — OIG's June 2026 companion report covers LTAC/IRF at the three largest MAOs); (3) reframe SNF authorization as the wedge into post-acute revenue integrity and show the expansion TAM explicitly.

### S2. There is no moat, and the deck's "why now" argues against itself
The most defensible thing this company could build is payer-portal data acquisition — brittle, unglamorous, genuinely hard to replicate. CMS-0057-F standardizes that away and hands it to everyone free on January 1, 2027. The mandate presented as the tailwind removes the only potential technical barrier. No accumulating asset is named anywhere: no proprietary dataset, no network effect, no switching cost beyond abandoning a spreadsheet.

The one credible candidate — a proprietary outcomes corpus (plan / denial reason / clinical argument / appeal stage / outcome) that makes winnability prediction better with every case — is hinted at by `outcomes.html` and absent from the pitch. Build the deck around it, and reposition 2027 honestly: it lowers your integration cost and everyone else's, so the window to accumulate a defensible dataset is the eighteen months *before* it opens.

### S3. Slides 5 and 13 contradict each other
Slide 5 sells the regulatory floor as strong enough that facilities can win on it now. Slide 13 mitigates the risk that CMS stops short-interval reauthorization by arguing "enforcement historically lags rulemaking by years." Both cannot be true. If the duration rule is enforceable, the 3-day cycle is already non-compliant — and that cycle is the deck's central pain metric (slide 3), the product's default configuration (slide 7), and one of three appeal templates. If enforcement genuinely lags, the legal floor sold to facilities is a rule plans can ignore.

Collapse it into the honest and stronger version: the rule is real and binding, but it is adjudicated case by case, per resident, only when the facility affirmatively invokes it — which is precisely why a facility needs a system to invoke it at scale. Rewrite both slides to that single story. And stop defaulting the product to a 3-day cycle as though it were legitimate; make short-interval reauth a **flagged compliance exception the product detects and challenges** (see C6, which supplies the argument).

### S4. The appeal-rate inference is the load-bearing one, and it runs backwards
The business model assumes software that lifts appeal volume from 18% toward 100% captures the 95% win rate. But the 95% is inframarginal — facilities appeal what they expect to win. Newly-surfaced marginal appeals have an unknown and almost certainly lower win rate. Worse, the step the pitch skips: what determines whether an appeal wins is skilled-need documentation. Slide 6 concedes this in its own wording ("assembles the regulatory argument around the facility's **clinical record**"). If the record is thin, no deadline engine rescues it.

The plausible reasons the other 82% go unappealed are (a) nobody has the hours and (b) the chart will not support it. Neither is a visibility problem, and Cardinal Coverage supplies neither the labor nor the documentation. Reframe slide 4 to **"82% of SNF denials are never appealed — that, not the win rate, is the addressable gap,"** state plainly that the expected win rate on newly-surfaced appeals is unknown and is the single most important thing the pilot must measure, and then either put concurrent skilled-need documentation prompts on the roadmap or disclose documentation quality as a precondition you assume rather than supply.

### S5. "Nobody currently sits here" is an unbounded universal negative with no evidence behind it
*Two agents, one of which was the dedicated competitive lane and could not run a single external check — WebSearch exhausted, all vendor and search domains proxy-blocked. This lane must be re-run with a fresh budget before any meeting.*

The only cited basis is four vendors' own marketing pages. There is no competitor log, no dated search record, no vendor URLs. `docs/gap-analysis.md:42` nonetheless certifies that the insight "survives contact with the competitive research" — research that does not exist as an artifact — and `README.md` adds "hedging that slide costs credibility," actively discouraging the qualification the claim needs.

The competitive set also populates only one quadrant of its own two-axis framing, omitting the categories that are prospective and upstream **by construction**: provider-side prior-authorization automation, AI denial/appeal-letter startups, hospital UR/UM vendors moving down-market, post-acute care-transition platforms, and SNF therapy documentation vendors. The scan appears to have been run on the founder's vocabulary rather than the market's — the materials never use "denial management," "utilization review," "revenue integrity," or "authorization management."

Actions: (1) rerun the scan on the market's terms and on Crunchbase/PitchBook for 2024-2026 rounds tagged post-acute + revenue cycle; (2) build `docs/competitive-landscape.md` as a dated log including dismissals; (3) replace the universal negative with **"We have not found a vendor selling SNF-specific, prospective MA authorization deadline management — NOMNC, QIO fast-track, and recertification clocks — as a primary product. The closest adjacent categories are X, Y, Z, and here is why each stops short."** The likely defensible claim is narrower than "nobody sits here" and closer to "nobody runs the NOMNC/QIO clock for SNFs" — appeal-letter software is a decades-old category; only its SNF-MA application is new.

Two specific corrections regardless of what the scan finds. The counterparty paragraph names **Carelon and only Carelon**, omitting what is very likely the largest and most litigated post-acute MA utilization-management operator (Optum/naviHealth) plus CareCentrix — the exact error the slide's own speaker note calls "costly." And "payers will not build the provider-side counterweight" is stated as a law when Optum operates post-acute UM and provider-facing RCM inside the same corporate structure, and criteria vendors (MCG, InterQual) license to both sides. Downgrade to a tendency and put "a both-sides platform extends into post-acute" on the risk slide.

Add the BPO threat path too: the deck treats outsourced prior-auth firms purely as a demand signal, but a BPO that productizes its internal tooling becomes this product with two advantages you lack — signed SNF contracts and an existing relationship with the business office manager. That is more probable than PointClickCare building a NOMNC clock. Also name two or three BPO firms and what a facility pays them; that would be the deck's best willingness-to-pay evidence and it currently has none.

### S6. The risk slide omits the two risks the company's own analysis calls fatal
`investors.html` lists EHR incumbents, regulation easing, SNF margins, compliance burden. Missing: **adoption/double-entry**, which `gap-analysis.md` calls the thing that "kills most SNF point solutions"; and **concentration** — one founder, no technical co-founder, no clinical lead, pilot inside the founder's own facility. Add both with real mitigations. Volunteering them buys the same credibility the slide already claims for the compliance point; a diligence process that reads the gap analysis will find the founder knew and left them off.

### S7. The related-party pilot is treated as pure upside
If the founder's facility is simultaneously design partner, pilot site, pricing anchor, outcome data source and reference customer, then first revenue is related-party revenue and first outcome data is self-graded. Disclose the structure on the slide: ownership relationship, facility-one revenue excluded from reported ARR, two external design partners committed before the round closes.

### S8. Nothing attributes recovered dollars to the software
A buyer can verify days were recovered; they cannot verify your software recovered them. Facilities already appeal and already win 95% of what they appeal — the baseline is not zero and the deck proposes no counterfactual. Design attribution into the pilot **now**: staggered rollout across units, or a matched prior-year baseline of appeal rate and win rate. And report **appeal-rate lift** as the headline metric — it is directly attributable, it is what the OIG data licenses you to claim you can move, and it is not confounded by case mix.

### S9. The 52%/93% comparison is a structural artifact
Traditional Medicare has no prior authorization for SNF admission, so there is no admission decision to appeal — the appeal category is definitionally near-entirely MA. Anyone with Medicare fluency will say so out loud. The honest version is already in the deck and buried in a small tile: 48% reviewer disagreement under MA vs 42% under Traditional Medicare, same reviewers, same standard. That is a real like-for-like comparison and it survives scrutiny. Promote it; cut or explicitly label the 52/93.

### S10. The NC plan-concentration claim carries the whole GTM and has no source
"A small number of dominant MA plans — so plan-specific workflow built once serves the whole state" is the reason the beachhead is claimed to be capital-efficient, and it is the only GTM claim with no number, no named plan, and no citation. It is also the most checkable: CMS publishes MA enrollment by state, county and contract monthly. Name the top three NC MA plans by enrollment with the CMS penetration file as source, state what share of NC SNF authorization volume they represent, and identify which UM vendor they delegate SNF utilization management to. If concentration is high this becomes one of the strongest slides. If not, pick a different rationale — one QIO region and founder proximity are legitimate on their own.

### S11. Missing commercial and legal paper
No privacy policy, no terms of service, no BAA template, no security overview page for a compliance officer (the sibling product ships one), no pilot agreement or MSA, no LICENSE, no roadmap, no financial model, no data room — despite the closing slide asking the founder to link one. `gap-analysis.md` discusses HIPAA as an engineering problem and never names the paper a facility asks for before the engineering is relevant. Add a "Gap 0 — paper, not code" section. These are days of work and they gate the first sales conversation the landing page is soliciting right now.

### S12. The messaging framework breaks its own rule
`SNF_MEDICARE_ADVANTAGE_MESSAGING.md`. The vocabulary is authentic — sticky notes, the third call to the plan this week, cut letter, covered days, NOMNC — and the pain cards read like someone who has sat in a business office. Then lines 96-106 instruct the seller to sort the buyer into visual/auditory/kinesthetic and lead accordingly. VAK representational-system matching is discredited NLP with no empirical support, and its presence signals the framework came from a sales-technique library rather than from the building. More damaging: line 120 ("No unverifiable claims. Every outcome in step 5 must be traceable to a product capability or a real customer result") is violated by the page built from it — all six step-5 outcomes ship on index.html against zero customers and several unbuilt capabilities. Cut the VAK section; keep the pacing structure and the guardrails; then enforce line 120 literally by marking each outcome shipped / roadmap / unmeasured and putting only the shipped ones in present tense.
