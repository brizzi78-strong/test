Why now

Regulation just handed facilities a legal floor — and is about to hand us the data pipe.

The leverage — already in force

  • CMS-4201-F (Jan 2024). MA plans must apply Traditional Medicare coverage criteria; no more restrictive internal guidelines.
  • 42 CFR 422.138. Authorization must stay valid as long as medically necessary, keyed to the treating provider's recommendation.
  • 42 CFR 422.626. On a termination appeal, the burden of proof sits with the plan.
  • Revised NOMNC/DENC forms (2025) expanded enrollee fast-track appeal rights.

The clock — CMS-0057-F

  • Jan 1, 2026 (now). MA plans must decide in 72 hours expedited / 7 calendar days standard, with a specific reason on every denial.
  • Mar 31, 2026. Plans must publicly report prior-authorization metrics — denial rates become public data.
  • Jan 1, 2027. Every MA plan must run a production Prior Authorization FHIR API, plus Provider Access and Patient Access APIs.

The rules changed in the facility's favor; the facility's workflow did not. And in twelve months the manual portal-chasing that makes this hard becomes an API integration — which rewards whoever already owns the facility-side workflow when the pipe opens.

Sources: CMS-4201-F; CMS-0057-F Interoperability and Prior Authorization Final Rule; 42 CFR §§ 422.138, 422.624, 422.626; AHCA/NCAL on the Medicare Advantage Improvement Act of 2026.

Say: This is the timing slide — spend real time here. Two arguments: the legal leverage exists today and nobody has tooling for it, and the 2027 API mandate turns our hardest technical problem (getting status out of payer portals) into a solved integration. If asked "why hasn't PointClickCare done this": the leverage is two years old, the appeal data is months old, and their auth tracking is a billing field, not a deadline engine — next slide.

The product

One dashboard. Every resident. Admission through discharge.

  • Authorization status and covered days for every resident, current, in one view
  • Deadline engine — next submission, NOMNC deliver-by, QIO fast-track cutoff, physician recertification, weekly progress note
  • Appeal workflow that assembles the regulatory argument around the facility's clinical record
  • Financial exposure by resident and by plan, current as of this morning
  • Shared across the team — nursing, therapy, MDS, and the business office read the same case

Appeal letter builder

Competition

Everyone works the claim after it is denied. Nobody works the stay while it is happening.

EHR platforms — PointClickCare, MatrixCare

Do record authorization numbers, coverage dates, and authorized service types against a resident, and can surface an expired auth before a claim goes out. That is a billing data field: it catches the gap at claim time. It does not run a NOMNC clock, a QIO fast-track deadline, or an appeal.

RCM analytics — SNF Metrics, primeCLAIMS, Inovalon

Aggregate reimbursement and AR data across facilities, report on denials and collections. Retrospective by design: they tell you how much you lost last quarter. They do not help you keep it during the stay.

Outsourced prior-auth services

BPO firms staff the authorization work for you. Real demand signal — facilities are paying humans to solve this today — but cost scales linearly with volume, and the facility still owns the missed deadline.

Cardinal Coverage

Prospective, during the stay: the deadline engine and the appeal workflow. The layer between the chart and the plan — where the money is actually won or lost, and where no one currently sits.

The category boundary: everyone else is downstream of the denial. We are upstream of it.

Their investment in systematising the denial is the demand signal for systematising the defence.

What we are watching

The honest risk list.

EHR incumbents move first

PointClickCare and MatrixCare could add authorization tracking. Mitigation: depth of the appeal workflow and regulatory argument, which is not a checkbox feature, plus speed in the beachhead.

Regulation eases the pain

If CMS forces plans to stop short-interval reauthorization, urgency drops. Mitigation: the documentation and deadline burden persists regardless, and enforcement historically lags rulemaking by years.

SNF margins limit budget

Facilities are financially strained. Mitigation: price against recovered revenue so the product funds itself, and sell to the person who owns the denial number.

Compliance burden

Handling PHI requires BAAs, encryption at rest, audit logging, and access control. Mitigation: budget it as a first-class line item in the raise, not an afterthought.

Say: Volunteering the risk list buys credibility and takes the adversarial edge off the Q&A. The compliance point especially — showing you know the HIPAA cost signals you have thought past the demo.
