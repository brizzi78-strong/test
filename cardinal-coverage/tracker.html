Fast-track appeal (North Carolina)

BFCC-QIO is Acentra Health · Phone 1-888-317-0751 · Fax 1-844-878-7921 · TTY 711. Voicemail accepted 24/7; translation available. Serves AL, FL, GA, KY, MS, NC, SC, TN.

The resident or their representative should call by noon the day before the NOMNC effective date. The tracker computes this, and cross-checks it against the 42 CFR 422.626 clock that runs from the date the notice was delivered.

If the deadline is missed, still call. Since January 1, 2025 (CMS-4205-F, amending 42 CFR 422.624/422.626) the BFCC-QIO — not the plan — must accept and decide an untimely fast-track request, and an enrollee no longer forfeits the appeal by leaving the facility. What a late filing loses is the guaranteed decision timeframe and the automatic financial-liability protection, not the review itself.

Against 3-day authorization cycles — 42 CFR 422.112(b)(8)

Under CMS-4201-F, approval of a prior authorization request for a course of treatment must remain valid for as long as medically necessary to avoid disruptions in care, in accordance with applicable coverage criteria, the individual patient's medical history, and the treating provider's recommendation. Codified at 42 CFR 422.112(b)(8) (Access to services / continuity of care) — not at 422.138. Verify the exact codified wording on eCFR before quoting it inside a letter.

Because duration is keyed to the treating provider's recommendation, state the ordered duration explicitly in the plan of care ("PT 5x/week × 21 days"). That makes every 3-day grant a documented departure from a physician's order. Request the clinically indicated duration and make the plan shorten it in writing.

A separate provision, 42 CFR 422.138(c), bars the plan from later denying an approved service on medical-necessity grounds, or reopening the decision absent good cause or fraud — and that protection covers concurrent determinations made during the stay, not just up-front prior auth.

Notice rules — an expiring auth is not a valid termination

If coverage is ending, the resident is owed a NOMNC (CMS-10123) at least 2 calendar days before the effective date — the last covered day — with QIO appeal rights. A plan cannot use a lapsed short authorization to skip the notice. If the QIO takes the appeal, the plan must issue a DENC (CMS-10124) by close of business that day.

Why appealing is the default

Acentra's CMS-approved study of its region: MA members are ~52% of the Medicare population but generate 93% of SNF appeals; reviewers disagreed with the denial 48% of the time; 27% of MA non-coverage notices were repeats within the same stay (avg 2.7 each, one resident received 12 after winning prior appeals); and on repeat appeals reviewers sided against the plan 77% of the time. HHS OIG (2026) found MA organizations overturned nearly all appealed SNF admission denials.

Documentation that survives review

Prior level of function stated concretely (not "independent"). Objective measures (Berg, TUG, gait speed, distance + assist level, Section GG). Functional, time-bound goals tied to the discharge environment. Daily notes showing what the therapist did — clinical decision-making, cueing type, parameters modified, safety judgment — never cloned. Explain every missed day. If plateaued, write the Jimmo maintenance rationale; improvement is not required for skilled coverage.

Escalation

1-800-MEDICARE and your CMS regional office for plan complaints. NC SHIIP (NC Dept. of Insurance) for beneficiary-side help: 855-408-1212 · ncdoi.ncshiip@ncdoi.gov. 42 CFR 422.137 requires each plan's UM committee to annually review policies for consistency with Traditional Medicare — a standing 3-day cycle is what those audits look for, so document the pattern.

Deadline conventions used here. "Covered through" is the last covered day — which is the Effective Date printed on the NOMNC (CMS-10123). CMS defines that date as the last day the beneficiary receives covered services, not the first uncovered day. NOMNC deliver-by is 2 calendar days before it. The deadline printed on the notice is noon on the day before it. Separately, 42 CFR 422.626 requires the fast-track request by noon of the first day after the notice was delivered — the same date only when the notice went out at the two-day minimum. Where they differ, this tool shows the earlier and colours urgency from it. Confirm against each plan's current notice instructions; these are calculated aids, not legal determinations.

Recertification caveat. The recert clock counts calendar days from admission. CMS counts covered Medicare days — leaves of absence and non-covered days do not advance it. Treat the recert date as an early warning and confirm against the covered-day count in your MDS record.
